The strangest detail of Anthropic's biggest week is the price cut. A company about to ask public investors for a $1.404 trillion valuation launched Fable 5.2, its top model with leading benchmark scores, and charged less for it.

Companies that think the model is the moat don't do that. Anthropic is behaving like a company that expects frontier intelligence to get cheaper every quarter, and wants to own the place where people actually use it. That place, as of Tuesday's San Francisco event, is a spreadsheet, a whiteboard and a Google Meet call.

Read the IPO and the product launch together. Treated separately, both make less sense.

## The numbers, without the hype

Here's what the amended S-1 says. Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78, raising $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion. It trades on Nasdaq under "ANT" around November 19. A separate $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock goes to Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, with Google and Nvidia putting in $10 billion each. If underwriters exercise the 123.75 million-share greenshoe, net proceeds reach $93.7 billion.

For scale, the largest IPO in history so far is Saudi Aramco's $25.6 billion in 2019. Alibaba raised $25 billion in 2014 and Facebook raised $16 billion in 2012. At the top of its range, Anthropic's public offering alone is about 2.5 times Aramco's.

The valuation went up and also came down. It's well above the $965 billion from the May round. It's also well below the roughly $2 trillion Anthropic was reportedly targeting a couple of months ago. That's a 30% haircut from its own ambitions, taken during what TechCrunch calls "a broader pullback from AI investing." The bankers had to meet the market partway, and that tells you the market can still say no.

Then there's revenue. Run-rate revenue hit $81 billion at the end of October, up about $10 billion from the end of September. That's roughly 14% growth in a single month at a scale most software companies never reach in total. At $1.404 trillion, Anthropic is priced at about 17x run-rate. That's expensive for software and cheap for a company adding a mid-cap SaaS business every month. Your view of the deal depends almost entirely on whether you think that monthly increase is durable or a spike.

## Why an office suite, and why now

Claude Code made Anthropic. Developers were the beachhead because they adopt fast, pay for tools and can tell immediately whether a model is good. But there are perhaps 30 million professional developers in the world and several hundred million knowledge workers. Nicholas Lin, who works on product at Anthropic, told The Verge plainly that non-developers are "the gap we're hoping to close."

So now there's Claude Sheets, with tabs, formulas, tracked AI edits and Excel export. Claude Whiteboards clusters ideas and drafts action items. Claude Meetings joins Google Meet as a visible participant and works while people talk. Claude Data, the former Dashboards, is now generally available. These sit alongside Docs, Slides, Design and Motion. Interactive connectors put HubSpot, Adobe and Atlassian apps inside Claude, built on an MCP ecosystem of more than 9,000 plugins.

Anthropic isn't first to any of this. OpenAI shipped its own office-suite tools on September 29. Meta launched Spaces for its Muse agent on November 6. Gemini already takes meeting notes in Meet, and Microsoft has spent three years putting Copilot into every Office ribbon. Anthropic is the fourth or fifth company to arrive.

What sets it apart is the starting point. Microsoft and Google begin with documents and add AI on top. Anthropic begins with the agent and builds documents around it. When Claude drafts a timeline on a whiteboard during a meeting, pulls last year's figures from a connected app, and turns the board into a deck after the call, the file is just a byproduct. Microsoft can't copy that design without making its own Office franchise less central. That's Anthropic's opening, and the main reason the suite matters more than the model.

## What nobody is talking about: the supply chain bought in

Look at who is in the $26 billion placement: Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC. Those are the GPU makers, the custom-silicon designer, the memory supplier and the foundry. Every layer of the chips Anthropic will spend this money on is putting cash in at the IPO price.

You can describe that as strategic alignment. You can also describe it as vendor financing with extra steps. Suppliers invest $26 billion, Anthropic spends a large share of its roughly $90 billion on compute, and the money comes back as revenue for the investors. Telecom equipment makers did a version of this with carriers in 1999 and 2000, and it ended badly. The difference is that Anthropic's customers are real and paying, at $81 billion a year. Still, public investors buying Class A shares should understand that some of the demand for this deal comes from companies whose revenue depends on Anthropic continuing to spend.

The second story getting too little attention is talent. According to SaaStr's reading of the filing, current employee options and RSUs are worth about $216 billion net at $78. Another 2.05 billion shares, about $160 billion, are reserved for future grants. Headcount went from about 940 at the end of 2024 to about 5,300 now, so the average stake is around $41 million. That average is skewed, though. The roughly 1,000 pre-2025 employees hold options with a $2.91 average strike, and many of them have nine-figure positions.

The $160 billion reserve is a hiring budget no private competitor can match. When the lockup expires, probably around May 2027, about a thousand people will become financially independent at once. Some will stay. Many will start companies, and the next wave of AI startups will be funded by former Anthropic employees with liquid stock. Anthropic is building a competitor ecosystem along with its product.

## The bear case, taken seriously

First, governance. Anthropic is a public benefit corporation. Founders hold Class F stock with 50.1% of voting power, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a board majority. Class A buyers are effectively passive passengers. That may be fine for safety-minded governance, but it means shareholders can't push management if the office suite underdelivers.

Second, GAAP. The filing shows about $32.3 billion of unrecognized RSU expense, and Anthropic says RSU expense will rise in the quarter the IPO closes. The first quarterly report will likely carry a very large stock-comp charge. Run-rate revenue is a marketing number, while GAAP losses go into headlines.

Third, distribution. Claude Meetings works only in Google Meet, which belongs to a competitor that is also an investor. Meetings also leaves automatically when an external guest joins, unless an admin allows it. That's a sensible privacy default, but it excludes client calls, sales calls and vendor calls, which are a large share of high-value meetings. Microsoft has Teams preinstalled across most of the Fortune 500. Anthropic currently has neither Teams nor Zoom.

Fourth, the price cut can be read in reverse. If Fable 5.2 has to be cheaper to win share, model margins are being compressed by OpenAI, Google and the open-weight labs. The suite is the hedge, but it's beta software competing with products that have decades of switching costs.

I still come down on the bull side, cautiously. Developers adopted Claude Code because it did real work, not because it was bundled with anything. If Sheets and Meetings deliver the same "it actually finished the task" experience, distribution becomes less important. If they don't, Anthropic is a $1.4 trillion model vendor in a market where model prices keep falling.

## What to Watch

1. **Pricing day.** I expect ANT to price at or above $78 and open well above that, especially with up to 20% of shares going to retail. A first-day pop above 30% means the range was set too low, and it will push OpenAI to speed up its own listing.
2. **Teams and Zoom support for Meetings by Q1 2027.** Without them, Meetings stays a feature for Google Workspace companies. Microsoft will be in no hurry to make Teams integration easy.
3. **First earnings: watch gross margin, not revenue.** Revenue will look strong. The real question is whether margins hold after the Fable 5.2 price cut and with compute spending funded by suppliers.
4. **Seat pricing for the suite.** Today Sheets and Whiteboards come with paid plans. Within two quarters, I expect a separate workplace tier priced to compete directly with Microsoft 365 Copilot's $30 per seat.
5. **May 2027 lockup expiry.** Watch for a wave of startup announcements from former Anthropic staff, and for how much of the $160 billion reserve Anthropic spends keeping its best people.

The IPO is the headline, but Claude Sheets is the product that will decide whether the valuation holds up.
