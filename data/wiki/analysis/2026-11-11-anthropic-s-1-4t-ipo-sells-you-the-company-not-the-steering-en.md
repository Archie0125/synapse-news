Anthropic's IPO is not a bet on the best AI model. Public investors can't fully use Anthropic's best model, Mythos 5.2, because the company is gating it. They won't control the company either. What the ticker ANT actually offers is exposure to a bet that Claude can take over the white-collar office from Microsoft and Google. Both of those companies are also writing Anthropic billion-dollar checks this month.

That's the story. The $1.4 trillion headline is the least interesting part of it.

## The numbers, briefly

According to the amended S-1, Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78. That raises $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion, and pricing is expected around November 18, with Nasdaq trading close to November 19. At the top of the range the fully diluted valuation is $1.404 trillion. Six months ago, in May, the company raised money at $965 billion. A couple of months ago it was reportedly aiming for $2 trillion.

Add a $26 billion private placement and the underwriters' option on another 123.75 million shares, and net proceeds come to $84.6 billion to $93.7 billion. Up to 20% of the offering goes to retail investors.

The revenue supports a big number. Run-rate revenue reached $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion in a single month. Q3 revenue was $17 billion. OpenAI just crossed $10 billion in a quarter for the first time and is at a $50 billion run rate. At the top of the range, Anthropic is priced at about 17x run-rate revenue. A software company growing this fast would usually get a higher multiple, so on that one measure the IPO looks cheap.

There's a catch. Anthropic counts sales that go through its cloud partners at full value. OpenAI reports only its share of some partner sales. The headline gap between the two companies is real, but part of it comes from accounting choices. If you compare $81 billion to $50 billion without adjusting for that, you're overstating Anthropic's lead.

## The placement deal

Look at who is in the $26 billion private placement: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC. Google and Nvidia are putting in $10 billion each.

Every company on that list sells Anthropic chips, memory, cloud capacity or fabrication. They're buying non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price, and a large share of that money will come back to them as compute orders. You can call that alignment, and partly it is. It's also a circular arrangement in which suppliers fund demand for their own products, and it makes the order book look stronger than independent demand alone would justify.

TechCrunch's earlier coverage described Anthropic as "still grappling with the economics of compute." The placement doesn't fix that problem. It moves the risk onto the balance sheets of the AI supply chain. If model pricing collapses, as cheaper open-weight models keep threatening, Nvidia and TSMC lose twice: once on the stock and again on the orders.

## The office suite matters more than the S-1

The S-1 is getting the attention, but the San Francisco product event says more about where Anthropic is heading:

- **Claude Sheets**: collaborative spreadsheets with tabs, formulas and tracked edits from Claude. It exports to Excel. It's in beta for all subscribers.
- **Claude Meetings**: a meeting agent that joins calls, takes notes, and answers questions afterward. It launched on Google Meet first, for Team and Enterprise customers.
- **Claude Whiteboards**: group brainstorming where Claude sorts ideas by theme and assigns action items.
- **Claude Data**: the renamed Dashboards, now rolling out to all paid users.

Anthropic's first wave of revenue came from developers, through coding and the API. This launch is aimed at the analyst, the project manager and the person in finance who lives in spreadsheets. Spreadsheets and meetings are where Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace make their money. Claude Meetings launching inside Google Meet, which already ships its own Gemini meeting agent, is about as direct a challenge as you can make on a partner's own product.

So Google is investing $10 billion in a company that is competing with Workspace on Google's own platform. Microsoft is investing while Claude Sheets goes after Excel users. Both companies are hedging: they'd rather own part of the winner than be shut out. For Anthropic it's a good position, as long as both partners keep tolerating it.

Look closely at one rule in the Meetings product. Claude won't join a call with outside guests unless an admin allows it, and it leaves if one joins anyway. That isn't a technical limitation. It's legal caution built into the product, and it shows what enterprise AI looks like in practice. The model is no longer the hard part. Consent, liability and compliance are, and Anthropic is building for the compliance team before the end user. That's why Anthropic wins enterprise deals, and it's also why it may never feel as fast as consumer-first products.

## Governance: you're paying for the ride, not the wheel

The founders hold Class F supervoting shares with just over 50% of the vote. TechCrunch reports the figure as both 50.1% and 51.1%. The Long-Term Benefit Trust also elects a board majority. Class A buyers get economic exposure and almost no say.

Each of the seven co-founders is selling 3,378,378 shares, about $250 million each at the $74 midpoint, or roughly $1.75 billion together. Afterward they still hold about 22% of Class A and keep majority voting control. Board member Reed Hastings wants to buy $300 million of stock through his donor-advised fund.

**The bull case** for this structure is not naive. A public benefit company that has said from the start it will slow down when safety demands it can't let a quarterly activist force a faster release schedule. Founder control plus the Trust is how Anthropic tries to keep that promise after going public.

**The bear case** is just as clear. "We must keep control for safety reasons" can't be falsified from the outside. Meta's dual-class shares were defended with "long-term vision," and Snap went public with no voting shares at all. Investors accepted both and later complained. When a model release costs Anthropic revenue or prevents a loss, public shareholders will have no way to tell whether the decision was principled or just commercial.

This is where Mythos becomes a governance issue and not only a product decision. Anthropic is asking the public for $60 billion while keeping its most capable model gated. I think gating frontier capability is defensible. Here is the angle I haven't seen anyone cover: once you're public, every access decision for Mythos is a disclosure question. Who gets access, on what terms, and with what revenue attached? Those will be material facts. So far "safety gating" has been decided internally. From now on, the SEC and plaintiffs' lawyers will be asking about it too.

## The employee equity problem

SaaStr's analysis of the S-1 puts current employee options and RSUs at about $216 billion of net value at $78. Spread across about 5,300 employees, that averages roughly $41 million each. Most of the option value belongs to about 1,000 people who joined before 2025, with an average strike of $2.91. That's a 27x spread to the top of the range.

Another 2.05 billion shares, about $160 billion, are reserved for future grants. That's about 11% of the company's valuation set aside to keep paying engineers.

That creates two effects investors should watch:

1. **Attrition after the lockup.** Hundreds of early employees will have life-changing money once the lockup expires. Some will leave to start companies, and AI startup funding in 2027 will be full of former Anthropic staff.
2. **Dilution and expense.** The filing lists $32.3 billion of unrecognized stock-based compensation and says RSU expense will spike in the quarter the offering closes. Expect an ugly Q4 GAAP loss and a lot of "adjusted" metrics to explain it.

## What to Watch

- **Pricing will come in at or above $78.** With Hastings buying $300 million, a supplier-backed placement and 20% going to retail, the setup favors the top of the range. I expect a first-day close at least 25% above the offer price, then a slide into the first earnings report.
- **Claude Meetings will reach Zoom and Microsoft Teams by mid-2027.** Launching on Google Meet is a beachhead. Teams is the real target, and how quickly that integration ships will show how much friction Anthropic can create with its Microsoft partnership before it breaks.
- **Mythos access will become a paid tier.** Within two quarters of listing, gated access will be sold as a premium enterprise offering with its own revenue line. "Safety gating" and "price discrimination" will be the same thing under different names.
- **OpenAI will file an S-1 or a confidential draft within six months.** Losing Q3 revenue $17 billion to roughly $10 billion while Anthropic sets a public benchmark is pressure OpenAI's investors won't accept for long.
- **The first proxy season will test the Long-Term Benefit Trust.** Watch for an institutional investor to file a shareholder proposal asking for more disclosure about how the Trust picks directors. It will fail, and the vote count will show how much the market actually trusts the "safety first" structure.
- **Supplier earnings calls will start calling Anthropic out by name.** Nvidia and TSMC now own Anthropic stock. Expect analysts to ask whether part of their AI revenue is just their own investment coming back as orders.
