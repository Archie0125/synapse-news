The Q1 2026 earnings season is over, and the verdict is unambiguous: the four hyperscalers that have bet most aggressively on AI infrastructure are now spending more than ever before — and they just told investors they plan to spend even more.

Analysts at Evercore and Bank of America placed their 2026 estimates even higher, between $800 and $900 billion.

More striking still: Alphabet explicitly said its 2027 capex will increase significantly compared with 2026. CNBC reported that some Wall Street analysts now project the combined AI infrastructure bill could **exceed $1 trillion** in 2027 — a figure that would have seemed fantastical just three years ago.

## Company by Company: The New Capex Landscape

**Alphabet** revised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to **$180–190 billion**, up from a prior range of $175–185 billion. The increase came alongside record revenue and backlog growth in Google Cloud: $20 billion in revenue, up 63% year over year, with a backlog that nearly doubled to $462 billion in a single quarter. CFO Anat Ashkenazi said 2027 capex would "significantly increase" relative to 2026, without providing a specific figure. Alphabet's stock rose almost 7% in after-hours trading.

**Microsoft** said it expects to invest $190 billion in 2026, with roughly $25 billion of that attributed to higher component pricing. Microsoft said Azure and other cloud services grew 40%, and CFO Amy Hood said margins on its AI business have remained better than cloud margins were at a similar stage. Microsoft's shares were essentially flat in after-hours trading after the report.

**Amazon** held its previously announced capex budget at **$200 billion** for the year — more than any other hyperscaler — while reporting AWS revenue of more than $37 billion for Q1, up 28% year over year. CEO Andy Jassy said the company is "confident in the long term capex investments we're making." Amazon shares rose after the report on strong cloud growth.

**Meta** raised its 2026 capex range to **$125–145 billion**, up from its prior range of $115–135 billion, explicitly citing higher component pricing and additional data center costs. CEO Mark Zuckerberg said every sign the company sees gives it confidence in the investment, but the stock fell more than 6% after hours as investors focused on the scale of its AI spending plans.

## The Question of Returns

The central tension this earnings season was the **credibility of returns on AI investment**. Google provided the clearest answer: its Cloud revenue grew 63% in Q1, versus 28% at AWS and 40% at Azure and other Microsoft cloud services. For Google, the capex story is relatively straightforward: the AI investment is directly traceable to cloud and search revenue acceleration.

Amazon's return story is harder to tell in the near term. AWS's 28% revenue growth is strong by historical standards but softer than Google Cloud's 63% and Azure's implied AI-driven gains.

## The Nvidia Effect and the Risk of Overcapacity

That logic has been validated quarter after quarter — but it also carries embedded risk.

The bear case, held by some investors and analysts, is that companies are pouring money into infrastructure that runs too far ahead of demand. The bull case, reflected in Alphabet's post-earnings stock jump, is that demand for AI compute keeps growing and justifies the buildout.

## What Comes Next

With Q1 earnings behind them, the hyperscalers now face the six-month question: can Q2 and Q3 revenue growth keep pace with the accelerating capex? The market gave its preliminary verdict in after-hours trading: Alphabet rose almost 7%, Meta fell more than 6%, and Microsoft was essentially flat.

Beneath the quarterly drama, the structural reality is that the AI infrastructure spending wave has now moved beyond a point of easy reversal. The $725 billion figure for 2026 is an estimate, and some analysts now see more than $1 trillion in 2027.
