OpenAI is preparing to confidentially file a draft of its IPO prospectus with the US Securities and Exchange Commission as soon as Friday, for a public listing it is reportedly aiming to hold in September, though that timing could shift. OpenAI is working with banks including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley on the draft prospectus. The company's last private valuation stood at $852 billion, set in a funding round of $122 billion in committed capital reported on March 31, and a listing could reportedly value it at up to $1 trillion.

The timing of the filing is conspicuous. The preparations come days after an advisory jury found on Monday that Elon Musk waited too long to sue OpenAI, a finding the judge immediately adopted. Musk had alleged the company went back on its vow to keep it a nonprofit. The lawsuit had been a legal hurdle for OpenAI's path to an IPO.

## What a Confidential Filing Actually Means

A confidential IPO filing allows a company to send draft IPO documents to the SEC for private review instead of disclosing them right away.

The timeline is aggressive but achievable if the SEC review process goes smoothly and market conditions remain favorable.

The confidential process gives OpenAI several advantages.

## The Financials That Will Define the Offering

Public market investors will scrutinize OpenAI's prospectus through several lenses. The positive signals are significant: OpenAI's most recently revealed run rate is at least $24 billion, and revenue has risen from about $2 billion in 2023 to over $20 billion by 2025. The company says ChatGPT now has over 900 million weekly users.

The challenging signals are also real. OpenAI's capital requirements are climbing, and the company still faces questions about whether its revenue growth will match its rising data-center spending commitments. At its current trajectory, the company will need either dramatically higher revenue or a structural reduction in compute costs before it reaches sustainable profitability.

Whether public market investors will accept the cash-burn profile that venture investors have tolerated is the central uncertainty of the offering.

## The Post-Musk Legal Landscape

The ruling on Monday removed what had been a major legal hurdle to OpenAI's public offering. The case, in which Musk alleged that OpenAI's leaders went back on their vow to keep the company a nonprofit, put Altman's leadership in the spotlight.

The jury's finding — that Musk's claims were time-barred — did not adjudicate the underlying merits of his argument that OpenAI had drifted from its safety-first origins. But it closed the legal avenue through which that argument could have disrupted the company's structure. For the IPO, the practical effect is decisive: the PBC conversion can proceed, the governance documents can be finalized, and the prospectus can describe a stable, legally settled corporate structure.

With the lawsuit dismissed, all resources can now focus on the offering itself.

## The Race With Anthropic

OpenAI is reportedly aiming to go public before Anthropic, which is also preparing for a possible listing in late 2026. The first of the two major frontier AI labs to reach public markets will set the valuation benchmark for the other. If OpenAI prices above $1 trillion and the stock performs well in the opening weeks, Anthropic benefits from a favorable comparable. If OpenAI's offering is received poorly — due to cash burn concerns, governance skepticism, or a market-wide risk-off environment — Anthropic may find its October window more complicated.

The two companies are not direct competitors in the IPO sense, but their offerings will be compared. Investors who participate in OpenAI's IPO will be making implicit judgments about Anthropic's comparable valuation, and vice versa. The AI IPO wave of fall 2026 will effectively define how public markets price the frontier AI category.

SpaceX, which was set to publicly disclose its IPO prospectus as soon as Wednesday and could go public as soon as June 12, adds a third high-profile offering to the pipeline.
