The race to ring the bell at America's stock exchanges just got a second, equally formidable entrant. On June 1, Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, formally beginning the process of taking the Claude maker public. The filing came less than a week after the company raised a $65 billion Series H funding round that pushed its valuation to $965 billion, above OpenAI's $852 billion valuation from March.

OpenAI is also expected to file for an IPO, setting the stage for an IPO season that will pit the two largest AI labs against each other. This time, the stakes are measured in the trillions.

## Revenue That Defies Precedent

The headline numbers are genuinely unprecedented for a company at the pre-IPO stage. Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate has crossed $47 billion — up from $9 billion at the end of 2025.

Growth has been fueled by enterprise adoption of Claude models for coding and agentic workflows. Claude Code is Anthropic's popular coding assistant.

## The $965 Billion Question

At its current valuation, Anthropic is approaching the trillion-dollar threshold. Whether public investors will accept that price tag remains to be seen, since the company has not yet made detailed financial information public.

The company, like all frontier AI labs, operates with structural cost challenges. Training and serving the largest Claude models requires enormous compute infrastructure. Last month, Anthropic struck a deal with SpaceX to use available compute at its Colossus 1 data center in Memphis, Tennessee, paying $1.25 billion per month. According to SpaceX's prospectus, Anthropic will pay that amount through May 2029, and either company can terminate the agreement with 90 days of notice.

Investors backing the Series H — a who's-who of institutional capital including Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, Sequoia Capital, Capital Group, Coatue, and D1 Capital Partners — are betting that Anthropic can thread that needle before it trades on a public exchange.

## Claude's Expanding Product Surface

If Anthropic follows through, it will file an S-1 registration document containing detailed information about its financials, legal matters, risks, and who holds the most voting power.

Notably, Anthropic previewed Mythos in April but has kept access restricted, warning that the model had discovered thousands of high-severity bugs that would need to be fixed before it could be made public. How Anthropic characterizes the safety and deployment timeline of its next-generation models in the public filing will be closely scrutinized by both investors and AI governance advocates.

Claude's model family has also been expanding along the capability-cost frontier. This product breadth mirrors OpenAI's strategy with the GPT family and positions Anthropic to capture spending at every tier of the market.

## Joining the $3 Trillion IPO Race

Anthropic is now the third major tech player expected to list in 2026, alongside OpenAI and SpaceX. Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX are all heading toward public markets in one of the largest tech IPO waves.

The sequence matters. OpenAI is expected to file for an IPO as well, with one report saying it confidentially filed around May 22 while another says it is still readying its confidential filing.

The confidential filing process lets a company begin preparing for a potential public offering without publicly disclosing detailed financial information, risks, or other internal business details. If Anthropic follows through, it will file a public S-1 with full financials, risk factors, and ownership details, which must reach investors at least 15 days before a roadshow.

## What Investors Will Watch

When the public S-1 drops, several questions will dominate analyst coverage. First, how much of that $47 billion ARR is actual annual contract value versus trailing run rate extrapolated from a few exceptional months? Revenue constructed from a handful of massive contracts with unusual consumption spikes can be misleading. Second, how concentrated is Anthropic's customer base? Heavy reliance on a few enterprise anchor customers creates churn risk. Third, what is the competitive moat as Google, Microsoft, and Meta all race to produce models that match Claude's coding and reasoning benchmarks?

For now, the confidential S-1 filing is a signal: Anthropic believes the window is open, the revenue story is compelling enough to withstand public scrutiny, and it intends to be a public company before the current AI market enthusiasm cools.
