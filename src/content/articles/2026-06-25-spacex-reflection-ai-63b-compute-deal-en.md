SpaceX has added another outside AI customer. On June 22, 2026, CNBC reported that Elon Musk's rocket company has signed a computing power agreement with **Reflection AI**, the $25 billion open-source frontier lab, worth up to **$6.3 billion** if the contract runs through 2029. Reflection will pay SpaceX **$150 million per month** starting July 1, 2026, gaining immediate access to Nvidia's top-of-the-line **GB300 chips** on SpaceX's Colossus infrastructure.

The deal is another signal that SpaceX's ambitions in AI infrastructure are real.

## Colossus Is Now a Cloud Platform

SpaceX built Colossus in part to power Grok, Musk's AI chatbot. By mid-2026, it has evolved into something that looks far more like a commercial cloud. SpaceX has already struck compute-related deals with Anthropic, Google and Cursor, which it is now acquiring, and now Reflection AI. The company is now selling computing power capacity to outside AI companies, positioning itself alongside cloud providers and AI infrastructure companies.

The Reflection contract also carries flexibility unusual for enterprise compute agreements: either party can terminate with 90 days' notice after the initial three months. That structure acknowledges the volatility of frontier AI development—training runs get cancelled, architectures change, competitors leapfrog—while locking in near-term revenue for SpaceX regardless.

## What Is Reflection AI?

Reflection AI was founded in March 2024 by **Misha Laskin** and **Ioannis Antonoglou**, two researchers with unusually strong pedigrees. Laskin led reward modeling for Google DeepMind's Gemini project; Antonoglou co-created AlphaGo. Their pitch is that the right AI talent can build frontier models outside established tech giants, and that enterprises and sovereign states want open models they can control.

Seven months before its $2 billion round, the company had been valued at $545 million. It later raised **$2 billion** at an **$8 billion valuation**, with investors including Nvidia among many others. It has since been valued at **$25 billion**, and it is working with the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission and has been part of broader Pentagon AI efforts.

The paradox of Reflection's position is striking: the company has not yet released a public frontier open-source model. Yet it commands a $25 billion valuation and has now committed to paying SpaceX $150 million a month for compute access. The bet being made—by investors including Nvidia—is that the demand for sovereign, open-weight US frontier AI is large enough to justify building the model first and worrying about monetization second.

## The Compute Sovereignty Thesis

The strategic logic behind Reflection's existence is closely tied to the geopolitical fracture in AI. After Anthropic cut off access to Fable and Mythos, open-source AI gained momentum, raising questions about the risks of relying on closed-model providers.

The argument: governments and enterprises are reassessing dependence on closed AI systems and want models they can inspect, customize and run with more control. Reflection, if it delivers weights that match frontier closed-model quality, would be the only product that satisfies both constraints simultaneously.

That is a large and currently unserved market. It is also, however, a market that assumes Reflection can actually build a frontier model—something it has not yet done publicly. The GB300 chips at Colossus are the hardware necessary to attempt it at the required scale.

## SpaceX as AI Infrastructure Kingmaker

The broader implication of the Reflection deal is the accelerating transformation of SpaceX's business model. The company, which recently completed a record initial public offering, has positioned itself as more than a launch provider and Starlink operator. It is becoming an AI infrastructure company, with Colossus as its flagship product.

For investors and policymakers, this raises questions that were not on the agenda when SpaceX was primarily a space company.

What is clear is that Reflection AI has decided the access is worth $150 million a month. With immediate access to the GB300 chips, the company will have more compute capacity to pursue what it calls "American open intelligence." The open-source AI race just got another serious contender.

## What to Watch

Reflection's first public model release is the most important near-term event to track. Reflection has not yet released a public frontier open-source model, so its first release is the most important near-term event to track.

If the model lands in Q3 2026 and benchmarks competitively with GPT-5.5 and Fable 5, the $25 billion valuation will look prescient. If it underperforms, or if the company cannot keep pace with the release cadence of well-funded competitors, the $150 million monthly commitment to SpaceX becomes a very expensive infrastructure experiment. For Elon Musk's balance sheet, either outcome is lucrative. For the open-source AI ecosystem, the stakes are considerably higher.
