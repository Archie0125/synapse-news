On July 22, 2026, Samsung took over central London for its annual Galaxy Unpacked event, announcing six new products in a lineup that spans its most refined foldable phone ever to its first AI-powered glasses. The announcements are designed to hold the high end of the Android market against Apple and Meta's growing spatial computing ambitions, while showing that Samsung's partnership with Google remains one of the most consequential in consumer tech.

## The Z Fold8 Ultra: When 4mm Is a Statement

The headline hardware is the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Samsung's most ambitious attempt yet to make the foldable form factor feel uncompromised. When open, the 8-inch main display measures just 4.1mm thick—a figure that required a new manufacturing process Samsung is calling Flex Titanium, which reduces the crease visibility that has long been the category's most persistent complaint. Folded, the device carries a 200-megapixel primary camera, a 5,000mAh battery, and the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 processor, all at 215 grams.

The starting price of $2,099 is steep but consistent with the category's positioning as aspirational hardware. Samsung is betting that buyers who have been waiting for a foldable that genuinely looks premium in the hand will find the Fold8 Ultra's combination of thinness, camera capability, and processing power persuasive enough to justify the premium over a conventional flagship.

The standard Galaxy Z Fold8, which starts lower, takes a different approach to display design. Samsung redesigned the aspect ratios—10:16 for the cover display and 4:3 for the main panel—to optimize each surface for different content consumption patterns: the cover for vertical scrolling and quick interactions, the inner screen for productivity and media. The Fold8 weighs 201 grams, four grams lighter than the Ultra.

## Z Flip8: The Compact Contender

For buyers who want the foldable premium without the book-size form factor, the Galaxy Z Flip8 makes a strong case. At 6mm thin and 180 grams, it is the lightest device in the lineup, running on Samsung's proprietary Exynos 2600 rather than Snapdragon. The expanded FlexWindow—the outer display visible when the phone is folded—gets new interaction modes that Samsung is positioning as a response to the iPhone's Dynamic Island, giving users access to notifications, quick controls, and app widgets without opening the device.

## Galaxy Glasses: Samsung and Google's Shared Bet on Android XR

The product that attracted the most attention at the London event was not a phone. Galaxy Glasses, developed jointly with Google on the Android XR platform, represent Samsung's entry into the AI-powered eyewear market at exactly the moment that Meta's Ray-Ban collaboration has proven the category has genuine consumer demand.

The design work was divided between two eyewear brands: Gentle Monster, the Korean luxury accessories label, and Warby Parker, the American direct-to-consumer pioneer. The collaboration with both signals Samsung's intent to appeal to fashion-conscious buyers across different markets and price sensibilities, rather than repeating the mistake of launching smart glasses that only appeal to early-adopter technologists.

The glasses run Gemini AI, enabling real-time translation of spoken language, contextual assistance based on what the wearer is looking at, and Lens-style object identification. The 9-hour battery life is competitive with Meta's current Ray-Ban smart glasses. Unlike some competitors, Galaxy Glasses launch without an integrated display—the focus is on audio and AI assistance rather than augmented-reality overlay, which Samsung's product team clearly concluded is still too technically and aesthetically immature to ship at consumer scale.

General availability for the glasses is set for later in 2026, with no firm date or pricing announced. Pre-orders for the phones and watches opened immediately following the Unpacked presentation.

## Watch Line: Health Features Take the Lead

Samsung also updated its wearable line with the Galaxy Watch Ultra2 and Galaxy Watch9. The Ultra2 features a 5,000-nit brightness display—a figure that matters for outdoor readability—and a 35% larger battery than its predecessor. Most significantly for fitness enthusiasts and professional athletes, it is the first Galaxy Watch rated for diving at 10 atmospheres, a specification that extends its use case well beyond casual water resistance.

The Galaxy Watch9 centers its pitch on health monitoring, with the headlining feature being FDA-cleared sleep apnea detection. Samsung has reorganized the watch's health interface around five wellness pillars—a reframing designed to make the device's accumulated sensor data more actionable for users who are not medical professionals but want to understand their health trends over time.

## The Bundle Strategy

Every device purchase comes with a six-month Google AI Pro trial including 5TB of cloud storage. The bundle is a meaningful competitive move: it pairs Samsung's hardware with the full suite of Google AI features at a moment when cloud storage and AI assistance are both increasingly central to how people use mobile devices. For buyers already invested in Google's ecosystem, the included trial lowers the switching cost from competing platforms.

## What Samsung Is Signaling

The six-product spread from Unpacked 2026 tells a coherent strategic story. On phones, Samsung is doubling down on hardware refinement—the Flex Titanium crease reduction, the thinner profiles, the lighter weights—because the company has concluded that the remaining barrier to foldable mass adoption is not capability but feel. On wearables, the Galaxy Glasses collaboration with Google is an explicit bet that Android XR can establish itself as a credible alternative to Apple's visionOS ecosystem before Apple ships its own glasses-class device.

The deeper question is whether Samsung can hold the premium segment as the competitive environment intensifies. Apple is expected to ship its own AI-enhanced glasses within the next 12 to 18 months. Meta's second generation Ray-Ban collaboration continues to gain momentum. And Chinese manufacturers including Huawei are closing the hardware quality gap in foldables faster than Samsung anticipated two years ago.

General availability for the full lineup is August 7, 2026. At $2,099 for the Fold8 Ultra, Samsung is clearly not aiming for volume—it is aiming to define what premium Android hardware looks like in the era of ambient AI.
