When Apple rings the bell on its fiscal third-quarter 2026 earnings call at 5 p.m. ET on July 30, something else rings with it: the end of an era. Tim Cook will take his seat as CEO of the world's most valuable technology company for the last time on an earnings call, addressing analysts and investors before handing the keys to hardware engineering chief John Ternus on September 1. After 15 years building Apple from a single-product company into a services-and-silicon juggernaut worth more than $3.5 trillion, Cook exits at the top.

The quarter itself—expected to show roughly $109 billion in revenue and earnings of $1.89 per share—is projected to be the third consecutive record quarter for Apple, a testament to the iPhone 17 super-cycle and the sustained expansion of its high-margin Services segment.

## What the Numbers Are Expected to Show

Wall Street consensus puts Q3 revenue at approximately $108.9 billion, up 16% from the $94.0 billion Apple reported in the year-ago period. Earnings per share are forecast at $1.89, representing a 20% improvement over the $1.57 delivered in Q3 2025. Apple's own guidance called for net sales growth of 14–17% year-over-year and a gross margin in the 47.5–48.5% range.

For context, Apple has beaten consensus EPS estimates in each of its last four quarters, with an average surprise exceeding 7%. The bar is set meaningfully above the prior year, but Apple's execution has been reliably better than estimates for the past 18 months.

The iPhone 17 family—which launched in September 2025 with hardware design updates and Apple Intelligence features—has continued to drive upgrade activity well into its second full quarter on shelves. Analysts point to a combination of factors: pent-up demand from a weaker iPhone 15 cycle, new first-time buyers in Southeast Asia and India, and Apple Intelligence pulling premium-tier buyers toward the highest-margin models. iPhone revenue is expected to approach $48 billion for the quarter.

Services, Apple's fastest-growing and highest-margin segment, is projected to contribute approximately $26.5 billion—a 15% year-over-year gain—driven by subscriptions (Apple TV+, Apple One, iCloud+), App Store fees, and the expansion of Apple Pay and financial services. The segment's operating margins exceed 70%, making each incremental Services dollar far more valuable to shareholders than incremental hardware revenue.

## The AI Calculus That Differentiates Apple

Perhaps the most consequential subplot of this earnings call is what Apple will *not* be announcing: a massive capital expenditure increase. While Meta guided to $145 billion in full-year capex, Microsoft spent $24.7 billion in a single quarter on AI infrastructure, and Google's Alphabet warned investors it would spend aggressively through at least 2027, Apple has maintained what analysts now call a "capital-light AI strategy."

Rather than racing to build frontier AI models or operate massive GPU clusters, Apple has engineered a hybrid architecture. Apple Intelligence—its brand name for on-device AI features—runs primarily on Apple Silicon: the Neural Engine baked into every modern iPhone, iPad, and Mac. More computationally intensive requests are routed to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which uses Apple-designed chips (manufactured by TSMC) in a privacy-preserving setup where Apple itself cannot read user data.

The result is that Apple captures AI feature value—better autocomplete, image editing, summarization, personalized suggestions—without the recurring GPU electricity and capital costs that are currently compressing margins at its hyperscaler competitors. Gross margin guidance of 47.5–48.5% represents an expansion of roughly 150 basis points from a year ago, even as Apple deploys AI across its platform.

"Apple's differentiated approach means customers benefit from AI features that are both powerful and deeply private, with the computational cost borne by Apple Silicon rather than rented infrastructure," Cook said at an event earlier this year. The market has rewarded this positioning: Apple stock has gained 25% year-to-date and recently reclaimed the title of the world's most valuable company from Nvidia.

## China: The Variable That Always Matters

One of the most closely watched line items will be Greater China, which contributed $16.4 billion in Q3 2025 and has been under consistent pressure as Huawei regained ground in the premium segment. The quarter may show early traction from Apple Intelligence's recent clearance in China, where regulators approved a version of the feature suite that complies with domestic data requirements.

If China revenue shows even modest recovery, it would significantly strengthen the bull case for a sustained iPhone 17 super-cycle extending into the fiscal year 2027 iPhone 18 launch. A further contraction would raise questions about whether Apple's AI features can meaningfully shift purchasing decisions in a market where local competitors offer aggressive alternatives at lower price points.

## Cook's Legacy: The Quiet Revolution

When Steve Jobs introduced Tim Cook as Apple's new CEO on August 24, 2011, the company's market capitalization was approximately $350 billion. At Cook's farewell earnings call, Apple will report quarterly revenue nearly a third of that total 2011 valuation—in a single quarter.

Cook's tenure redefined what a technology company could be. He scaled Apple's supply chain to manufacture hundreds of millions of precision devices annually. He transformed Services from a supporting act into a $100+ billion annual revenue segment with margin characteristics that rival the most profitable software businesses in the world. He navigated the U.S.-China relationship through two administrations and a trade war while maintaining manufacturing relationships essential to Apple's cost structure.

On AI, Cook's critics will note that Apple was late. The Siri voice assistant, once ahead of its time, fell behind ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude on capability. Apple Intelligence launched in 2024 with features that seemed cautious compared to what Google and OpenAI were shipping. The privacy-first framing was partly strategic—Apple's architecture genuinely limits what it can do with aggregated user data—and partly a necessary response to competitive disadvantage.

Whether Apple Intelligence becomes a genuine AI differentiator or merely table stakes for the iPhone business is the defining question Cook leaves for his successor.

## Enter John Ternus

John Ternus, 51, has served as Apple's Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering since 2021, overseeing the development of Apple Silicon—the M-series chips that redefined Mac performance and enabled a unified hardware-software ecosystem across iPhone, iPad, Mac, and now Apple Vision Pro.

Ternus is arguably the best-informed person in the world about the substrate on which Apple Intelligence runs. He understands, at a level of detail few executives possess, what Apple's chips can and cannot do. That makes him a logical steward for an AI strategy that is fundamentally a hardware story.

His challenge is different from Cook's. Cook inherited a visionary product culture and needed to industrialize it. Ternus inherits a meticulously optimized machine and needs to determine what the next breakthrough looks like: whether Apple pushes deeper into ambient computing and glasses, whether it competes more aggressively in AI services, and how it manages an increasingly complex regulatory environment in the U.S. and Europe.

## What to Watch in the Call

Beyond the headline numbers, analysts will be listening for:

- **Guidance framing**: How conservatively does Cook set Q4 expectations, and does Ternus make any appearance on the call in preparation for the handoff?
- **China tone**: Any commentary on early Apple Intelligence traction in Greater China
- **Services mix**: Whether streaming and financial services are growing faster than App Store, which matters for regulatory conversations in Europe
- **Capex trajectory**: Any hint of whether Apple plans to increase Private Cloud Compute investment under Ternus
- **iPhone 18 cycle positioning**: How management discusses customer behavior heading into the fall launch window

This may be Tim Cook's last earnings call as chief executive, but it will set the tone for the next chapter. The numbers are expected to be excellent. The harder read is what comes next.
