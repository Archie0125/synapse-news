Developers of [virtual private network (VPN)](https://ssd.eff.org/module/choosing-vpn-thats-right-you) apps are [reporting](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/09/24/russia-crackdown-on-bypassing-online-censorship) that their products are again being pulled from Apple’s App Store in Russia. This is an unacceptable crackdown on a vital tool to protect privacy and speech online.

Why? VPNs give people a way to control their own online activities. When you visit a website through a VPN, that website only sees the VPN server's location (well, IP address) rather than your actual location. It's like sending a letter through a P.O. box so the recipient doesn't know where you really live. It can also disguise where you’re accessing information from, [which can make it possible](https://ssd.eff.org/module/understanding-and-circumventing-network-censorship) to access information banned by your government.

This is an unacceptable crackdown on a vital tool to protect privacy and speech online.

Going after tools that give people the space to communicate and access information freely is a common tactic from the totalitarian playbook. This latest demand comes after several steps the Russian government’s taken to surveil speech specifically on Apple products. In 2024, the [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2024/07/04/russia-forces-apple-to-remove-vpns-from-app-store-a85608) reported that Russia’s state media watchdog, Roskomnadzor, went after VPN apps on the Apple App Store, after banning access to Western social media platforms and blacklisting opposition media sites. Russia then [blocked Apple’s FaceTime software](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/12/04/russia-shutters-facetime-as-it-tightens-control-over-apps-communication) in 2025, according to Apple Insider.

[Bloomberg also recently reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/russia-adds-apple-and-microsoft-to-state-surveillance-registry) that the Russian government has added Apple and Microsoft to its state surveillance registry. This gives Roskomnadzor access to local users’ correspondence, [Apple Insider reported](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/29/russia-orders-apple-to-surrender-users-messages-on-demand), and puts both companies in a position to turn over customer information to the country’s security services as required.

With government control encroaching on their devices and less access to tools that give them alternate methods, people in Russia are left with shrinking options to communicate online or find information that isn’t state-sanctioned.

While Russia’s actions offer a more extreme vision of what happens when the government tries to enforce speech rules by attacking vital privacy tools, it is far from the only government doing so. Several [states in the U.S.,](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/11/lawmakers-want-ban-vpns-and-they-have-no-idea-what-theyre-doing) bent on making it difficult to circumvent invasive age-gating laws, have suggested banning VPNs—either to access certain content or in some cases to simply ban them outright. EFF has [publicly (and loudly) criticized](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/court-agrees-eff-utahs-vpn-law-demands-technical-impossibility) these proposals, because going after VPNs is a dangerous step down the road of censorship.

Attacks on speech and privacy online are deeply troubling, no matter where they happen. What those in power may see as a “loophole” in their invasive laws is actually an expression of user control. We’ll continue to speak out when we see governments going after them to take that control away.

## Summary

**TLDR:** Russia's VPN purge is predictable authoritarian behavior. The real question is how often Apple will keep complying, and what its App Store chokepoint means for everyone else.

- VPN developers report that their apps are again being removed from Apple's App Store in Russia.
- This follows Roskomnadzor's 2024 push against VPN apps and Russia's 2025 block of FaceTime.
- Bloomberg reported that Russia added Apple and Microsoft to a state surveillance registry, which escalates pressure on US platforms.
- VPNs hide a user's real IP address and help people get around censorship, which is why governments target them.
- A centralized app store gives governments one lever to cut off privacy tools for millions of people.
