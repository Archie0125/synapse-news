A memo circulated earlier this year claimed that technology executive Alice Huang was recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, according to multiple media reports.

The New York Times, which had previously said Nvidia’s chief executive had been the target of Chinese intelligence, was [the first to report on the memo](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/06/us/politics/chinese-spy-agency-nvidia-jensen-huang.html?unlocked_article_code=1.OVE.Q7K1.pw9O9sjsZBW4&smid=url-share), which was reportedly based on U.S. intelligence. [The Financial Times subsequently reported](https://www.ft.com/content/a55a2a22-4479-4f93-a445-6147a5c6d790?accessToken=zwAAAaG0GO1zkdOlWioiRHlPk9OkRWFHpcbXkA.MEYCIQD8tFSp3cRj0J5I30Q9RKgUzsTJxyEgT-Dwn1kE4QrvnwIhAPfAlYzUdcV9Q_kHHTf-UsaGXHM8kf4-86XcXPSpT3HD&sharetype=gift&token=44a48365-ecb3-4a02-8bb8-9eba3deab5e1&syn-25a6b1a6=1) that the memo also claimed Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at a Singapore-based company called Megaspeed.

An Nvidia spokesperson told the NYT that Jensen Huang’s interactions with Alice Huang were “not unusual,” and that the company does not share classified information with its customers.

A [separate NYT report](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/07/business/economy/nvidia-chips-chinese-firms.html?unlocked_article_code=1.OlE.MZ9x.zeop-X6IZe5B&smid=nytcore-ios-share) examined New Matrix, a Chinese company authorized by the U.S. Commerce Department to buy advanced Nvidia chips. But at the office address listed by New Matrix, reporters only found “a hollow gray shell,” raising concerns that it could be a front for a blacklisted private equity firm.

The Nvidia spokesperson said the company sells “the first, best and most of our technology in America.”

## Summary

**TLDR:** Spy rumors grab the headlines, but the bigger story is an approved Nvidia chip buyer whose listed office turned out to be an empty shell.

- A memo reportedly based on US intelligence claims that Megaspeed executive Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.
- The Financial Times reported that the memo also alleged a romantic relationship between the two. Nvidia says the interactions were not unusual and that it shares no classified information with customers.
- Separately, the NYT found that New Matrix, which the US Commerce Department authorized to buy advanced Nvidia chips, listed an office address that was a hollow shell.
- That raises concerns that New Matrix could be a front for a blacklisted private equity firm, which would point to gaps in export control enforcement.
- Nvidia now faces both national security scrutiny and reputational risk.
