Concerns are being raised that neither the Commerce Department nor Nvidia are doing their due diligence when it comes to vetting Chinese firms cleared to handle the company’s H200 AI chips. According to *[The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/07/business/economy/nvidia-chips-chinese-firms.html)*, New Matrix is on the list of approved firms, yet its offices are empty, “a hollow gray shell with wires dangling from the ceiling where lights should be.” In fact, the paper could find little evidence that the company exists in any meaningful way:

# The US let Nvidia sell chips to an empty office in China

The government is approving sales of Nvidia’s AI chips to Chinese firms with ties to blacklisted companies.

Only scant information can be found online about New Matrix, which was created 17 months ago. At the building that New Matrix lists as its address, a company directory does not include the firm. At the nearby property management agency for the industrial park, a receptionist and a manager both said they had no record of the company.


What little information can be found about New Matrix is concerning. Its owners have ties to a Chinese private equity firm that has been blacklisted by the US government since 2024. And according to the *Times*, this is not a rare lapse in oversight, but a troubling pattern of companies connected to entities with national-security-related trade restrictions being approved to purchase Nvidia chips.

Nvidia had been restricted from selling chips to China during the Biden presidency. But the Trump administration [relaxed those limits](/news/840557/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-exports-us) after Nvidia agreed to [give the government](/news/757428/donald-trump-nvidia-amd-ai-chip-sales-china) a [15-percent cut](/tech/781384/apparently-you-can-pay-your-way-out-of-national-security-concerns) of all sales. The company’s CEO, Jensen Huang, also launched an intense [lobbying campaign](/policy/996211/jensen-huang-trump-state-dinner) and was apparently targeted by China’s Ministry of State Security in a [spying plot](/tech/1029338/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-china-spy).

## Summary

**TLDR:** If an empty office can pass US export vetting for H200 chips, the controls are mostly for show, and a 15% revenue cut seems to have replaced due diligence.

- The New York Times found that New Matrix, a firm approved to handle Nvidia's H200 AI chips in China, operates from an empty, unfinished office and shows little sign of real activity.
- New Matrix was created 17 months ago, and its owners have ties to a Chinese private equity firm that the US has blacklisted since 2024.
- According to the Times, this is part of a pattern of approved buyers linked to entities under national-security trade restrictions, not a one-off mistake.
- The Trump administration loosened Biden-era limits after Nvidia agreed to give the government 15% of its China sales.
- The findings put both the Commerce Department and Nvidia's vetting under scrutiny, and make stricter oversight from Congress more likely.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** 連一間空辦公室都能通過美國審查、買到 H200 晶片，這套出口管制看起來只是做做樣子，15% 的分潤似乎取代了盡職調查。

- 《紐約時報》調查發現，獲准在中國經手 Nvidia H200 AI 晶片的 New Matrix，辦公室空無一物、天花板電線外露，幾乎看不出有實際營運。
- New Matrix 成立僅 17 個月，股東與一家自 2024 年起就被美國列入黑名單的中國私募基金有關。
- 《紐約時報》指出，這不是單一疏失，而是一再有和受國安貿易限制實體相關的公司獲准購買晶片。
- 拜登政府時期曾限制 Nvidia 對中國出貨，川普政府在 Nvidia 同意上繳中國銷售額 15% 後放寬了限制。
- 商務部和 Nvidia 的審查機制都受到質疑，國會加強監督的機率隨之升高。
