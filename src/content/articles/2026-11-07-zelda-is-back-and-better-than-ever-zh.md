## The Drop

- [***The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time***](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&xs=1&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.nintendo.com%2Fus%2Fstore%2Fproducts%2Fthe-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-125697%2F)**.** I have a confession to make: I’ve never played this game all the way through. Not sure how! Just missed it. But seeing my friends and coworkers fall back in love with Hyrule has convinced me to get all the way into the remake. I genuinely can’t wait.
- [***Godzilla Minus Zero***](https://www.themoviedb.org/movie/1381071-0-0?language=en-US)**.** Three years ago, *Godzilla Minus One* was a big hit and a surprisingly great movie. The sequel came with much higher expectations, but everyone seems to agree it’s a deserving follow-up. I’m not generally one for monster movies, but this series really does it for me.
- [**Affinity for iPad**](https://www.affinity.studio/ipad-app)**.** Canva’s app now unifies all its tools in one place, just like on the Mac. Affinity makes perfect sense on the iPad, and comes with lots of Apple Pencil integration and clever touch-friendly tools. I’m no designer, but I am excited to play with this as a photo editing tool.
- [**The Moft Dynamic Folio Case**](https://moftinc.pxf.io/c/482924/2764386/31698?u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.moft.com%2Fproducts%2Fdynamic-folio-for-iphone-duo%3Fvariant%3D44268934856791)**.** Here’s one for all you iPhone Duo buyers. Moft’s cases are a little complicated to figure out, but really cleverly designed to make your phone even more useful. I’m guessing this is going to be a *lot* better than Apple’s weird case / kickstand thing.
- [**The Peak Design Kickstand Travel Battery**](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&xs=1&url=https%3A%2F%2Fgo.skimresources.com%3Fid%3D87443X1797501%26xs%3D1%26url%3Dhttps%253A%252F%252Fwww.peakdesign.com%252Fproducts%252Fkickstand-travel-battery%253Firclickid%253Dwvi0ayVkSxyZWdfxKcxrVSZwUkry0nyZ03VeUM0)**.** While we’re on the subject of kickstands, here’s the one I’m buying this week. Peak Design makes good stuff, and I frequently find that I need extra juice at exactly the moment I want to watch something on a plane or train. This one’s going right into my backpack and probably never coming out.
- [**The Bambu Lab H3C**](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&xs=1&url=https%3A%2F%2Fgo.skimresources.com%3Fid%3D87443X1797501%26xs%3D1%26url%3Dhttps%253A%252F%252Fus.store.bambulab.com%252Fproducts%252Fh3c)**.** I am dangerously close to becoming a 3D Printer Dude. At $2,500, this one’s a little steep for my wallet, but I think [my colleague Sean Hollister is right](/tech/1024978/bambu-h3c-color-mixing-toolchanger-3d-printer-trend) that this is what the future of 3D printing looks like. Simpler, more foolproof, easy enough even for a goofus like me to make some pretty cool stuff.
- **“**[**Ranking the world’s most popular pens for journaling**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bYKPS5T4Blo)**.”** I know we all love a good pen — and Peter McKinnon does an excellent job of breaking down a few dozen supposedly good pens to see which ones really hold up. I bought more than one pen after watching this.
- [**Instagram Edits for Mac**](https://edits.instagram.com/desktop/)**.** Oh, great, just as I was finally getting good at CapCut! I like the Edits mobile app a lot, and the desktop app seems to be even a little more powerful. Plus: free. This is how I finally take down Cristiano Ronaldo.
- [**The Sony MDR-3R headphones**](https://www.tkqlhce.com/click-101881227-15268034?url=https%3A%2F%2Felectronics.sony.com%2Faudio%2Fheadphones%2Fheadband%2Fp%2Fmdr3r-s)**.** Respect to Sony for taking the retro headphone trend all the way to its logical conclusion and just releasing the headphones from the original Walkman. You’ll look a little like Nathan Fielder in *You Can See Everything* when you wear them, and $60 ain’t cheap, but there’s something so incredibly ’70s-era cool about them.
- [**The Motorola Signature 27**](https://motorolanews.com/motorola-signature-27-2/)**.** This is just a pure tour de force of a smartphone spec sheet. Monster camera; brightest screen; giant battery. Also very cool to see a flagship phone with support for GrapheneOS, for the less Google-inclined among us. It’s expensive, and only shipping… someday… but I just like that phones like this exist.

We talked last week about [**Readwise Books**](https://readwise.io/books), the new app that’s a mix of Kindle, Goodreads, and Strava. I’m a longtime fan and user of [**Readwise Reader**](https://readwise.io/read), so I wasn’t shocked that I liked the new app a lot, too. And I’ve heard from a bunch of you that you do, too!

I’ve talked to Tristan Homsi, one of the Readwise cofounders, a few times over the years, and have always enjoyed hearing his thoughts about reading, apps, and digital products in general. I asked him to share his homescreen to see if I might be able to get a hint about what he’s reading and the other stuff he thinks is good. (I also asked Daniel Doyon, Readwise’s other cofounder, to share, and he didn’t respond. Daniel is dead to me.)

Here’s Tristan’s homescreen, plus some info on the apps he uses and why:

**The phone:** iPhone 13 (!).Believe it or not, I chose to buy this relatively outdated phone last year… not because it’s my favorite form factor or anything, but because it’s closer in specs to the median device our Readwise users have and helps me build a bit more user empathy with them. I’m able to catch performance regressions and bugs in Readwise that the developers on our team with Duos and Pro Maxes and such miss.

Let me tell you though, iOS 26 itself does not work amazingly on an iPhone 13. Lots of lagging and janky animations. I am somewhat bought into the conspiracy theory that they make the old phones suck so you buy a new one… not so much intentionally, but rather by virtue of just making each successive iOS use more and more resources, doing fancier and fancier things.

**The wallpaper:** My lovely wife and my cat Sophie :)

**The apps:** Clock, Settings, Google Calendar, Camera, Google Maps, Spotify, Bay Wheels, Oura, Eight Sleep, Auto Eight, BeReal, ChatGPT, Runmeter, Strava, Readwise Highlights, Readwise Reader, Phone, Messages, Safari.

Of course, I have all three Readwise apps front and center. [**Highlights**](https://readwise.io/) and Reader as app icons, and Readwise Books in widget form at the top! It’s a great reminder to jump back into your current book (I’m reading [***Apple in China***](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Apple-in-China/Patrick-McGee/9781668053386) right now… speaking of Apple!) instead of scrolling twitter (on a later page), and seeing my friends currently reading really makes me want to jump in and join them :)

I also am a huge fan of the somewhat-defunct social app [**BeReal**](https://bereal.com/). It had its moment in 2022, but quite a few of my friends are still on there! You post once a day, can see what your close friends around the world are doing, and most importantly can’t get sucked in for more than two minutes since it limits you to a random photo or two a day! I still post basically every day, and have almost five years of memories in there.

Speaking of getting sucked in, I don’t have it pictured on my home screen, but I also have [**Clearspace**](https://www.getclearspace.com/) installed, which helps me stay off of all social media.

Some other apps shown on the homescreen: “auto eight” is something I vibecoded to better control my [**Eight Sleep**](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&xs=1&url=https%3A%2F%2Fgo.skimresources.com%3Fid%3D87443X1797501%26xs%3D1%26url%3Dhttps%253A%252F%252Fwww.eightsleep.com%252F) mattress (lol)… [**Strava**](https://www.strava.com/) and [**Runmeter**](https://runmeter.com/) are awesome for tracking my runs… and [**Bay Wheels**](https://lyftbikes.com/) is my main mode of commute around San Francisco daily.

*I also asked Tristan to share a few things he’s into right now. Here’s what he sent back:*

- I follow some amazing niche internet blogs, which you should check out if you haven’t. I love the blog [**Slime Mold Time Mold**](https://slimemoldtimemold.com/), famous for their citizen science and [potato-maxxing](https://slimemoldtimemold.com/2025/02/02/potato-riffs-retrospective/). I also love [**Gwern.net**](http://gwern.net/), [**Deepfates**](https://deepfates.com/cantrip), and [**Dynomight**](http://dynomight.net/)… among many others!
- And of course I’m always reading books. The most recent fiction series I *loved* was the [***Red Knight*** **series**](https://readwise.io/books/16303487/) — which I can only recommend if you are into dense, dark, fantasy — but man, if you are, the author is a genius.
- For non-fiction, it’s hard for me to plug anything but my favorite book of all time, [***The Beginning of Infinity***](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/24745/the-beginning-of-infinity-by-david-deutsch/9780140278163). It’s not the easiest to get through, but if you do it will likely change the way you see the world, as it did for me!

## Signing off

I’ve mentioned [**Kagi**](https://kagi.com/) a bunch of times in this newsletter — it has been my default search engine for the last few years, and I can’t recommend it highly enough. (What if Google, but good, you know?) The challenge with Kagi is it costs $10 a month, which is a lot to ask when Google is free. But as of this week, you can now at least try it for yourself, since Kagi will give you 50 free searches before even asking you to sign up. You get 100 more when you do, even before you pay.

If you search a lot, and miss what Google used to be, give Kagi a whirl. I am a very happy customer, and suspect I will be for a long time.
