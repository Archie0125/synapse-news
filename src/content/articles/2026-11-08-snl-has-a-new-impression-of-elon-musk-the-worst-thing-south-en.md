Saturday Night Live cast member Jeremy Culhane unveiled his impression of Elon Musk last night, depicting the SpaceX and Tesla CEO as an awkward loser who fumbles his attempt to befriend the hosts of Weekend Update.

This isn’t the first time the show has lampooned the world’s richest man ([and one-time SNL host](https://www.avclub.com/it-is-not-a-joke-that-elon-musk-hosted-saturday-night-l-1846854532)), with [Mike Myers stopping by occasionally](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CUpOMSJ1MdU) to wield a chainsaw before freezing and muttering, “Glitch!”

Since Musk was in the news this week for [spending half a billion dollars on candidates who lost in the midterms](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/07/after-the-midterms-things-are-looking-rocky-for-elon-musk-and-data-centers/), then [announcing plans to halt Cybertruck production](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/tesla-will-stop-making-the-cybertruck-in-2027/), it’s maybe not surprising that Culhane’s version is a little less obnoxious and a little more desperate to be liked, doing his best to laugh off his setbacks and remind the audience, “[My car did a flip](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/tesla-reveals-spacex-powered-roadster-that-flies-and-falls-with-style/)!”

Eventually, Weekend Update co-host Colin Jost asks a pointed question about Musk’s week: “You moved to Texas because California was run by Democrats. You moved all your companies to Texas because you didn’t want to pay taxes. You even built your own town in Texas. And now Texas is run by Democrats. Now you’re going to have to pay taxes. So how does that make you feel?”

Culhane nods along with each step of the question, then answers, “Bad?”

To show that he’s a good sport about all this, Culhane also asks his chatbot Grok to do “an epic vulgar roast of me, Elon Musk.” Grok delivers, describing Musk as “the worst thing South Africa has ever done” and suggesting, “Maybe if you have 100 more kids, [one of them will talk to you](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Wilson).”

SNL seems to have its knives out for tech executives this season, starting with [Jane Wickline’s impression of Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/27/anthropics-dario-amodei-gets-the-snl-treatment/) as a stuttering prophet of AI doom.

A couple weeks later, James Austin Johnson [caricatured data center backer/”Marty Supreme” co-star Kevin O’Leary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_M4FeYWkTuE). Asked about the widespread data center backlash, Johnson-as-O’Leary smoothly replies, “All I want is to buy up 40,000 acres of God’s untouched splendor and replace it with a very loud computer that steals your job.”

## Summary

**TLDR:** SNL's new Musk impression is less a sketch than a public read on his bad week: midterm losses, the end of the Cybertruck and his own chatbot roasting him.

- SNL cast member Jeremy Culhane debuted a Musk impression on Weekend Update, playing him as awkward and desperate to be liked.
- The sketch came the same week Musk spent half a billion dollars on midterm candidates who lost and Tesla announced it will stop making the Cybertruck.
- Colin Jost asked how Musk feels now that Texas, where he moved his companies partly over taxes, is run by Democrats.
- In the sketch, Musk's own chatbot Grok roasts him as the worst thing South Africa has ever done, using his AI product as the punchline.
- The shift from an obnoxious Musk to a needy one suggests pop culture now sees him as vulnerable rather than unstoppable.
