Almost seven years ago, while clutching a laptop, my phone, and a notebook in a sea of **Tesla** fans, I watched head designer Franz von Holzhausen throw a metal, baseball-sized ball at the driver’s-side window of the **Cybertruck**. The result was [cracked glass](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/21/tesla-accidentally-busted-two-windows-on-the-cybertruck-while-demonstrating-how-tough-they-are/) — actually, two by the end of the presentation — and some awkward jokes and chuckling by CEO **Elon Musk**.

I have witnessed a number of stunning, odd, and fantastical moments at Tesla events. But the [Cybertruck reveal](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/21/behold-the-tesla-cybertruck-is-here/) was the most interesting — and not because of the postapocalyptic design. It was the crowd.

When the truck rolled out onto the stage, I watched attendees gasp, some in revulsion. There was mumbling (“This certainly can’t be the *Cybertruck*?” I heard Tesla superfans whisper). And yes, there were cheers. The momentary shock of Musk’s biggest fans was quickly replaced by support, acceptance, and finally, endorsement.

It moved like a wave over the crowd. By the time attendees were waiting in line for Cybertruck rides, the mood had shifted. Everyone was a fan.

That fandom carried over to Cybertruck reservations, which skyrocketed to 1 million, [Musk](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2023/10/18/tesla-tsla-q3-2023-earnings-call-transcript/) said during a third-quarter earnings call in 2023. But it never made the leap to actual sales. Industry experts estimate about 71,000 Cybertrucks have been sold since the first deliveries began in November 2023.

And now the Cybertruck is headed to the scrap pile. Tesla will stop making the Cybertruck next year, Musk said Friday.

Was the vehicle too edgy? Maybe, but this was always going to be a niche market. I believe more practical reasons dampened sales. Musk promised the Cybertruck would have a 500-mile range and a price tag under $40,000. It achieved neither, senior reporter Sean O’Kane [noted in his report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/tesla-will-stop-making-the-cybertruck-in-2027/).

## Summary

**TLDR:** About 1 million reservations turned into roughly 71,000 sales. The Cybertruck shows that fan excitement at a launch event is not the same as demand.

- Tesla plans to stop making the Cybertruck, about seven years after its 2019 reveal.
- Musk said in 2023 that reservations had reached 1 million, but industry experts estimate only about 71,000 have been sold since deliveries began in November 2023.
- The 2019 reveal went viral partly because the supposedly unbreakable window cracked on stage, and the crowd went from shock to enthusiasm.
- Reservations cost little and are easy to cancel, so they measure hype rather than willingness to pay.
- The newsletter frames transportation through AI, and the Cybertruck's end suggests Tesla is shifting its focus away from niche vehicle programs.

- 預訂成本低、隨時能取消，衡量的是話題熱度，而不是消費者願意付多少錢。
