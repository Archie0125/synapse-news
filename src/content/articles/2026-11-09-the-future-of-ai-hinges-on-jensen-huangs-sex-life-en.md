# The future of AI hinges on… Jensen Huang’s sex life?

NVIDIOT.

NVIDIOT.

Alice Huang — no relation to Jensen — is a former TV reporter. That was before she became [“an executive” at Megaspeed,](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/megaspeed-nvidia-jensen-huang-alice-huang.html) which bought $4.8 billion of hard-to-get Nvidia chips, starting around the time she was being photographed with Jensen in a Taipei bar in 2024. “To many in the industry, the well-funded push by Megaspeed into A.I. appeared to come out of nowhere,” [*The New York Times* wrote, tactfully](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/06/us/politics/chinese-spy-agency-nvidia-jensen-huang.html). Our girl Alice is also an alleged honeypot who was recruited by the Chinese Ministry of State Security. ([China is denying all this,](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/09/business/jensen-huang-china-spying.html) btw.)

You know shit is going good when your corporate spokesperson has to tell the *Times* that *of course* your company does not disclose classified information to your customers

What is Megaspeed? Well. The company has ties to the Chinese military and builds data centers in “Southeast Asia” (it has offices in Singapore and Indonesia) to sell compute to Alibaba. The chips it is using to do that are banned from being imported to China. Incidentally, Nvidia has been lobbying very intensely to get rid of import controls on its chips so it can sell them in China.

This is all public knowledge in part because Secretary of State [Marco Rubio reportedly “oversaw” a memo](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/08/us/politics/rubio-nvidia-ceo-chinese-spy.html) detailing Alice’s recruitment and relationship with Jensen, and then someone leaked it. (Powerful memo game, Marco, getting credit for one you didn’t even write!) Rubio passed his little note around in May, ahead of a trip to China taken by President Donald Trump. Jensen initially didn’t make the list of invitees on the trip — at least, not until Trump saw media coverage of the perceived snub. Trump and Jensen? They’re close. Which makes it even more interesting that Trump was personally briefed on the findings and did nothing, according to the *Times*.

You know shit is going good when your corporate spokesperson has to tell the *Times* that *of course* your company does not disclose classified information to your customers. John Rizzo, the spokesman, also told the *Times* that Nvidia’s customers don’t ask Nvidia to lobby for changes to the chip restriction. Bit of a hat on a hat, John. First of all, they don’t have to — Nvidia’s financial incentives do it for them. Second, imagine this is a regular company. The *business model* of Megaspeed is that the chips are restricted, and that Megaspeed gets ahold of them. Megaspeed has no incentive to upend this situation! Unless, you know, it’s acting against its financial interest because it’s a front for Chinese spies… in which case, that statement is going to be embarrassing.

Speaking of embarrassing, Rizzo told the *Times* “that Mr. Huang’s interactions with Ms. Huang were ‘not unusual’ and consistent with the kinds of meetings he has with any of Nvidia’s many customers.” Oh, okay. Who else was Jensen fucking?

Nvidia has an *extraordinary* structure

Because two days after that statement, Jensen sent the most *incredible* email addressed “To my colleagues at Nvidia” in which he admitted he had been “unfaithful.” Then, he wrote, “To my wife and family: I love you. I know an apology cannot undo what I have done. I hope our family will heal and that, over time, I can earn back your trust.” Are… your wife and family on the Nvidia employee email list?

Well, yes! His son is a director of product management; his daughter is a senior marketing director. He even gave a job to his son-in-law!

Also, Nvidia has an *extraordinary* structure. Jensen has 60 direct reports. Leave aside that there is no possible way to manage that many people well. I would like to direct your attention to something else entirely: If he is in trouble, there is no part of the company that is untouched. The usual problems attain, of course — he was in a position to have gotten any information she might have requested! He could have left company laptops available for infiltration! But because of the structure, he *also* could have issued commands to his *many* direct reports that benefited the CCP. Neat! I sure hope someone who knows a lot about cybersecurity is going through Nvidia with a fine-toothed comb.

Is he in trouble? Well, Nvidia is opening an investigation into Jensen, who [is also its largest individual shareholder](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/insights/120816/top-3-shareholders-nvidia-corporation-nvda.asp). (At least [there’s no dual-class stock](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/08/2026/nvidias-45-trillion-test-of-founder-immunity)!) Nvidia shares are, as of this writing, down almost five percent, which seems like an underreaction.

Neoclouds exist because Nvidia wanted to create competition for its chips, so its biggest customers — Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Oracle — couldn’t exert too much pricing pressure. Certainly I am not the only person who has noticed that Nvidia is, effectively, [the central bank of AI](https://www.economist.com/interactive/briefing/2026/09/03/nvidia-is-the-central-bank-of-ai). If Nvidia is in a particularly bad way, it kind of seems like, at minimum, the neoclouds get fucked too. ## Summary

**TLDR:** The Megaspeed spy allegations matter less as gossip than as a stress test of Nvidia's lobbying against export controls; one leak story could undo that campaign.

- Reports link Jensen Huang to Alice Huang (no relation), a former TV reporter turned Megaspeed executive who was photographed with him in a Taipei bar in 2024.
- Megaspeed bought $4.8 billion in hard-to-get Nvidia chips. The New York Times reports she was allegedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, which China denies.
- Megaspeed reportedly has ties to the Chinese military and builds Southeast Asian data centers to sell compute to Alibaba, using chips banned from import into China.
- Nvidia says it does not disclose classified information to customers, while it lobbies hard to relax chip export controls on China.
- Whatever the truth about the relationship, the case gives export-control hawks a vivid example of chips leaking through third countries.
