Nearly a year ago, Waymo updated its self-driving software and issued a recall after numerous reports that its robotaxis were illegally passing school buses that had stopped and deployed flashing red lights. Now, federal safety regulators have opened a new investigation into why that behavior has continued.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) said in a November 6 [filing](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2026/INOA-RQ26006-19477.pdf) that a majority of these reported incidents occurred after the company deployed a software update intended to fix the problem.

Reuters was the [first to report](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-opens-probe-into-2895-waymo-vehicles-over-school-bus-safety-violations-2026-11-09/) the filing.

Waymo said last year that a [software update was completed](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/waymo-to-issue-software-recall-over-how-robotaxis-behave-around-school-buses/) for all affected vehicles on November 17, 2025. However, NHTSA said the majority of the incidents occurred after the software update. NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) has opened a “recall query” to “assess the adequacy of the remedy.”

The ODI opened its initial investigation into Waymo in October 2025 after seeing footage of one of its autonomous vehicles maneuvering around a stopped school bus that had its stop sign extended, lights flashing, and was unloading children in Atlanta. In that incident, a Waymo robotaxi crossed perpendicularly in front of the school bus from its right side. The autonomous vehicle then turned left around the front of the bus before traveling down the street.

Numerous other incidents have been documented in the weeks and months since in Austin, where Waymo also operates a robotaxi service. Austin Independent School District officials reported at least five such incidents at the time, [according to a letter](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2025/INOT-PE25013-30888P1.pdf) filed with NHTSA.

Reached for comment, Waymo stressed the importance of safety to its mission. “We have made significant improvements to vehicle behavior around school buses and have received no citations since March 2026,” a spokesperson told TechCrunch via email. “The Waymo Driver has a clearly demonstrated positive impact on roadway safety. Once received, we’ll work to respond to NHTSA’s query and look forward to continuing our engagement on recall processes for AV software that continues to learn and improve.”

TechCrunch also reached out to Austin Independent School District officials for comment and will update the article if they respond.

*This article was updated after publication with Waymo’s statement.*

## Summary

**TLDR:** Waymo's school-bus recall looks like a fix that worked on paper only, because NHTSA says most violations happened after the patch shipped.

- NHTSA opened a recall query into about 2,895 Waymo vehicles to assess whether the November 2025 software remedy was adequate.
- Per the November 6 filing, most reported incidents of passing stopped school buses happened after the update.
- The original probe began in October 2025 after footage showed a Waymo maneuvering around a bus unloading children in Atlanta.
- Austin ISD reported at least five similar incidents, so the problem was not limited to one city.
- "Recall via OTA update" only counts as a fix if it changes real-world behavior, and regulators now want proof that it does.

**重點摘要：** Waymo 的校車召回修正只在紙上有效，NHTSA 指出多數違規其實發生在更新推送之後。

- NHTSA 針對約 2,895 輛 Waymo 車輛啟動召回調查（recall query），評估 2025 年 11 月的軟體修正是否足夠。

- 奧斯汀獨立學區通報至少五起類似事件，問題不只出現在單一城市。
- 透過 OTA 更新完成召回，必須真的改變上路行為才算數，監管機關現在要看證據。
