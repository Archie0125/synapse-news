United says it [won’t let personal AI assistants book tickets](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=87443X1797501&xs=1&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.united.com%2Fen%2Fus%2Ffly%2Fhelp-center%2Fdigital-trust-center%2Fartificial-intelligence%2Fthird-party-ai-agents.html) on the airline, citing both privacy and regulatory concerns.

# United won’t let your AI agent book your flight

AI agents can’t attest that you don’t have explosives in your bags.

The company says it hasn’t yet seen many people try to use AI agents to book their flights, [*The Wall Street Journal* reports](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/united-airlines-is-banning-ai-bots-from-booking-flights-5b514b38), in line with observations from other businesses like [Doordash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/doordash-said-its-only-received-several-hundred-orders-from-ai-agents/). But United is concerned that agents could expose customers’ information or make purchases they didn’t intend, and potentially skirt federal rules about the kind of information airlines need to share with customers — like having them attest that their bags don’t have explosives.

While agents can still show users United flights, they won’t be able to actually complete the transaction on their behalf. The decision, and others like it, could limit the potential usefulness of newer personal AI assistants like [Meta’s Muse](/tech/993391/meta-muse-ai-hands-on) and [OpenAI’s Dots](/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002033/openai-dots-launch-muse-competitor). “To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman,” United CEO Scott Kirby said at an event on Tuesday, according to *the Journal*.

Across sectors, businesses are grappling with whether to let AI agents transact on their sites, and United is not alone in blocking access for unauthorized agents. eBay [appeared to block agents](/tech/1030838/ebay-ai-agents-meta-muse-agentic-shopping) from browsing the site, and [several major banks](/tech/1016768/ai-agents-are-causing-massive-headaches-for-banks) have also placed restrictions on them. United suggests it could reconsider its decision based on future advancements. “As third-party AI agents advance, we see the opportunities they present. However, at the same time, allowing them to interact directly with a United account or booking could involve sensitive actions, and we want to make sure we have established protections for travelers first.”

## Summary

**TLDR:** United's AI agent ban is framed as safety, but its CEO said the real fear out loud: becoming just another supplier behind an AI middleman.

- United Airlines will not let personal AI assistants complete flight bookings, though agents can still show users United flights.
- The airline cites privacy, unintended purchases, and federal requirements such as passengers attesting that their bags contain no explosives.
- CEO Scott Kirby said, 'I don't want to create another middleman,' which reveals a business motive alongside the regulatory one.
- eBay and several banks have also restricted agents, which limits the usefulness of assistants like Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots.
