The data center boom is running into serious real-world obstacles, from widespread [local opposition](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/texas-halts-new-data-centers-as-governor-calls-for-audits/) and permitting delays to a [scramble for electricity](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/08/the-biggest-u-s-power-grid-is-under-strain-from-ai-and-no-one-is-happy/). Before those challenges even come into play, though, there’s a more basic problem: A lot of the projects being announced may never get built in the first place. With [billions of dollars](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/20/the-best-ai-investment-might-be-in-energy-tech/) in AI infrastructure investment at stake, separating realistic projects from speculative ones is becoming increasingly important.

On this episode of TechCrunch’s [Equity](https://techcrunch.com/podcasts/equity/) podcast, hosts Rebecca Bellan and Tim De Chant are joined by [Brent Nelson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/brentanelson/), senior managing director of markets and strategy at [Ascend Analytics](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/about-us/leadership), to separate the data center projects that are likely to get built from the ones that may never get off the ground.

## Summary

**TLDR:** Many announced AI data centers are press releases, not projects, so the real bottleneck may be credibility rather than concrete.

- The data center boom faces local opposition, permitting delays, and competition for electricity.
- A more basic issue is that many announced projects may never be built at all.
- With billions of dollars in AI infrastructure investment at stake, telling realistic projects from speculative ones is increasingly important.
- On TechCrunch's Equity podcast, Brent Nelson of Ascend Analytics discussed which projects are likely to get built.
