[Fractile](https://fractile.ai/), a British startup developing AI chips, has raised [a $720 million Series C](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/we-are-delighted-to-share-that-fractile-has-ugcPost-7526285169943625747-n5p9/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAB0HLDwBOaW7CV4YcFXMiqWwgQvC-bwf2xA) backed by Britain’s Sovereign AI Fund with hopes of “ensuring the U.K. has a greater stake in the hardware that will underpin its future economy,” its government [stated](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sovereign-ai-and-british-business-bank-invest-in-british-firm-developing-the-next-generation-of-ai-hardware).

Alongside the likes of [Axelera](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/27/axelera-lands-new-funds-as-the-ai-chip-market-heats-up/), [Etched](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/etched-fields-funding-offers-at-40b-valuation-sources-say/), and [OLIX](https://olix.com/news/company-raises-series-b), this makes Fractile one of the top-funded startup players in the race for purpose-built AI chips. Such chips are one of several avenues being explored to reduce the cost of inference — aka using increasingly demanding AI models in production.

However, Fractile seeks to go beyond hardware with a full-stack approach that also includes a machine learning compiler to maximize the speed at which LLMs will get executed on its chips.

As a result, the company frames itself as a frontier AI company, and its latest round led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners, and Redpoint was priced accordingly. According to the British government, the valuation was “over £5 billion” — quite possibly $7 billion, a figure reported both [by the Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/f999a0c2-af52-499f-8008-accbdfd45a25) and [a Reuters source](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/uk-backed-ai-chip-startup-fractile-hits-7-billion-valuation-funding-round-2026-11-11/).

Still, Fractile’s main differentiator will arguably be its hardware design. After a [pivot](https://dealroom.co/news/talk-OpeCP4wCxkA-the-memory-bandwidth-bet-behind-fractiles-inference-chips/), its approach now revolves on DRAM, the most common type of RAM, but stacked on top of each chip in such a way that it is hoping will bypass the usual trade-off between memory and capacity.

This trade-off has become more problematic with the rise of agentic AI, as companies seek to perform longer chains of tasks with large models without compromising on speed. As compute struggles to keep up, hopes are high for AI chips, despite their limited availability to date.

In a [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7526304067573182464/), Fractile CEO Walter Goodwin wrote that “there is still a lot of work ahead” for the company, and that the new funding will enable the startup to “ship \[its\] first chips and systems next year.”

## Summary

**TLDR:** Fractile's $720M round is as much British industrial policy as a venture bet, and a roughly $7B valuation is a lot to prove with silicon.

- British AI chip startup Fractile raised a $720 million Series C backed by the U.K.'s Sovereign AI Fund and led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed, and Redpoint.
- The U.K. government put the valuation at over £5 billion, and the FT and a Reuters source reported about $7 billion.
- Fractile is building inference-focused chips plus a machine learning compiler, a full-stack approach it uses to frame itself as a frontier AI company.
- The raise places it among the best-funded AI chip startups alongside Axelera, Etched, and OLIX, as cutting inference costs becomes a central race.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** Fractile 這輪 7.2 億美元募資，既是創投押注，也是英國的產業政策；約 70 億美元估值要靠晶片證明，壓力不小。

- 英國 AI 晶片新創公司 Fractile 完成 7.2 億美元 C 輪募資，英國主權 AI 基金參與，由 Thrive Capital、Founders Fund、Lightspeed 和 Redpoint 領投。
- 英國政府表示估值超過 50 億英鎊，《金融時報》和路透消息來源則指約 70 億美元。
- Fractile 主打推論專用晶片，再加上機器學習編譯器，靠這套全端做法把自己定位成前沿 AI 公司。
- 這輪募資讓它和 Axelera、Etched、OLIX 並列資金最充裕的 AI 晶片新創；降低推論成本已經成為主要戰場。
