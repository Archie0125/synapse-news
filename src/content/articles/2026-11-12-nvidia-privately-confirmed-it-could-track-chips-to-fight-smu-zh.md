Representatives from Nvidia told Illinois representative Bill Foster that it would be technically possible to implement the anti-smuggling measures called for in the Chip Security Act, Foster told *MIT Technology Review* in an interview on Thursday, even while an industry group claimed they were “potentially infeasible.”

Foster, a Democrat who serves on the Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, is a cosponsor of the act, which would introduce geotracking requirements to verify the location of advanced AI chips in order to prevent their smuggling to China. It is one of three chip-security bills under consideration for inclusion in the Senate’s National Defense Authorization Act, each of which is drawing renewed attention after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation and reported affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

When the Chip Security Act was introduced, in May 2025, lawmakers said the technology to implement it existed, something Nvidia has not publicly confirmed. In March 2026, the Semiconductor Industry Association, of which Nvidia is a member, explicitly [opposed](https://www.semiconductors.org/sia-statement-on-chip-security-act/) the bill and wrote that its mandates for chip tracking were “new, untested, and potentially infeasible.”

But privately, Nvidia representatives working with Foster told the lawmaker that the company could implement them.

“They never put anything in writing,” Foster says, but during technical discussions on the possibility of such safeguards, their reaction was “yeah, of course we can do this.” This private acknowledgment to Foster goes further than anything Nvidia has previously made public.

Other technology Nvidia has built for its customers suggests that the company can indeed offer accurate tracking of its chips’ locations. In December 2025, for example, it [announced](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/optional-data-center-fleet-management-software/) a new tool called Fleet Intelligence that would allow customers to monitor how its advanced chips were performing across different data center locations. In doing so, the tool could indicate which country chips were in, Nvidia [confirmed](https://www.reuters.com/business/nvidia-builds-location-verification-tech-that-could-help-fight-chip-smuggling-2025-12-10/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) to Reuters, but the company would not elaborate on whether this technology could be used to prevent smuggling.

Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment or confirmation of Foster’s characterization.

In early October, when lawmakers were reportedly [increasingly concerned](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-01/nvidia-faces-questions-over-china-ai-chip-smuggling-cases?taid=6abef534a7b7100001e74694&utm_campaign=trueanthem&utm_content=business&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter) about Nvidia’s role in smuggling, *MIT Technology Review* asked the company if Fleet Intelligence could be used to prevent the practice. It declined to answer directly, instead sending a statement: “Governments and media have found insignificant diversion of controlled US products—a drop in the bucket compared to the ocean of domestic compute China already has.”

The recent fallout at Nvidia involves a number of people and companies alleged to be involved in chip smuggling. Alice Huang, with whom Jensen Huang reportedly had an affair, runs Megaspeed, a Singaporean firm now under investigation for smuggling advanced GPUs to China. A US Commerce Department inquiry into the company was [reportedly halted](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/09/business/economy/nvidia-megaspeed-investigation-huang.html) after invention by Nvidia. Megaspeed [reportedly](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/us-probing-if-alice-huang-helped-smuggle-super-micro-products) bought chips from Supermicro, a company that packages Nvidia’s chips onto servers.

The Chip Security Act as drafted would require a mechanism to track the locations of chips, either by inspections of facilities, attestations by companies, or ping-based location mechanisms. The ping-based technique, the hardest option to circumvent, would require the GPU to send a signal of its location when it comes online. In September, the Center for Security and Emerging Technology [found](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/tracking-ai-chips/) it would be the most cost-effective option.

Foster said he did not ask Nvidia’s representatives why they weren’t voluntarily adopting such chip-tracking tools if they could technically do so.

But if the requirements of the bill pass as part of the Senate’s National Defense Authorization Act, the new tracking mechanisms would only tell investigators where the chips are, not offer a way to disconnect them if it’s revealed they have been smuggled. Foster told *MIT Technology Review* he intends to introduce new, stricter legislation in the new year that would offer a workaround. The new rule would require licenses to operate advanced GPUs, and before a GPU could connect, its valid license would need to be verified.

In the meantime, the controversy at Nvidia, Foster says, has fueled support among his colleagues for the rules in the bill: “There will be a lot of political pressure, in the wake of everything that’s happened, on the companies to actually do a very good job of the geographic validation of where the chip actually is.”

## Summary

**TLDR:** The chip-tracking debate looks less like a technical impossibility than a political choice, though Nvidia’s unwritten private assurance still needs independent verification.

- Representative Bill Foster says Nvidia privately acknowledged that safeguards proposed by the Chip Security Act were technically possible.
- The Semiconductor Industry Association, which counts Nvidia as a member, publicly called the tracking requirements new, untested, and potentially infeasible.
- The proposed law would require geolocation checks for advanced AI chips to deter smuggling into China.
- Because Foster says the discussions were not documented, the disclosure raises policy questions without conclusively establishing Nvidia’s technical capabilities or official position.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** 晶片追蹤似乎不是做不到，而是業界願不願意做；只是輝達未留下書面承諾，仍須獨立查證。

- 眾議員 Bill Foster 表示，輝達人員曾私下承認《晶片安全法》要求的防走私措施在技術上可行。

- Foster 沒有取得書面確認，因此這項說法揭露了政策矛盾，卻還不足以證明輝達正式承諾能部署相關功能。
