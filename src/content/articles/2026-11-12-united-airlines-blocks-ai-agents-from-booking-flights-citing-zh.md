United Airlines said it will restrict access to AI agents seeking to book flights and manage reservations on their users’ behalf, citing a risk to cybersecurity and undermining federal regulations.

The airline is the latest American corporate giant to prevent customers from using AI agents and autonomous assistants to access their systems. The move comes as airlines and banks are struggling to differentiate between real customers and AI systems operating on their behalf, such as when users share their logins with their AI assistants. Sharing passwords with apps and assistants can create security risks if those credentials get leaked or exposed.

United warns that “sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles.”

The airline also warns of a risk of violating federal aviation rules, such as requiring that a human completes flight check-ins to acknowledge that their bags do not contain banned substances.

“As a result, United is taking steps to restrict access — directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators — to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants,” the airline said [in a statement](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496). “This work includes updates to our booking, ticketing and distribution policies.”

The airline’s move follows a warning from banks, including Bank of America, which last week urged [customers not to share their passwords](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/bank-of-america-kindly-asks-that-you-dont-share-your-banking-password-with-your-ai-assistant/) with their AI assistants.

## Summary

**TLDR:** United’s blockade shows AI agents are outrunning the trust infrastructure needed to let software safely act as a customer.

- United Airlines will restrict unauthorized third-party AI agents from booking flights or managing reservations for customers.
- The airline says sharing account credentials can expose personal data and enable unintended purchases, account changes, or MileagePlus redemptions.
- Automated check-in may conflict with federal requirements that a human acknowledge baggage does not contain prohibited substances.
- Restrictions will extend beyond United’s own platforms to travel agencies and aggregators through updated booking, ticketing, and distribution policies.
- The dispute exposes a wider need for secure delegated authorization rather than password sharing between customers and artificial intelligence assistants.

- 真正的產業缺口，是缺乏安全的代理授權機制，迫使使用者只能以共享密碼的危險方式讓人工智慧代辦工作。
