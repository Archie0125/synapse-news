[Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/), the maker of screen-less fitness bands, is launching a social network on Thursday that lets its members follow friends and family and share their health data.

The network, called Whoop Humans, will be rolled out in the Whoop app to members at every membership tier at no extra cost. Members can follow anyone on Whoop and choose what to share, from sleep and recovery to workouts and achievements. Followers can react and comment; members can also attach photos and commentary to their data. It’s all opt-in, CEO Will Ahmed tells us.

Whoop already has a social feature, Teams, which a little more than half its customers use, according to the company. But Teams is built around groups and leaderboards, and everyone in a group sees everyone else’s information. Humans is meant to be more intimate. Members decide who follows them and who sees what, so a trainer can track a set of clients without the clients seeing one another’s data, for example.

I found myself questioning how much a spouse wants to know about the other’s sleep score when Ahmed mentioned this particular scenario, so I asked him how else beta members used these newer social features. He said the company learned that people were more engaged when someone reported a low recovery score than a nearly perfect one, suggesting that the new feature may be particularly helpful for understanding when someone is struggling and might need attention.

Indeed, the bigger story here is where Whoop continues to be headed, which is into medical wellness. Like its most direct rival, Oura, the company is pushing faster and more aggressively into the wellness opportunity that an aging population presents.

Whoop had already added FDA-cleared ECG and atrial fibrillation detection, blood pressure insights, blood testing, and medical-record uploads. Asked if older customers are gravitating to Whoop, Ahmed said, without offering specifics, that Whoop has seen “huge growth” among people over 60 over the past 18 months. He also said his was among the first companies accepted into a Medicare program that [reimburses qualifying wearables](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/connected-care/) that can show they improve health outcomes, and that it now has Medicare members.

He added that he expects families to be big users of Humans. (You can imagine adult children checking in on aging parents.)

But the company’s efforts to be thought of as the “brand for personal health” raises questions, naturally, about privacy. Ahmed said members choose which metrics to share, can remove followers, and can switch on “stealth mode” to go dark for a few days without changing their settings. Activities marked sensitive aren’t shared by default, and Ahmed said health alerts and medical information aren’t shared at all. Members can also report any comment or photo that they deem disturbing, and the company said reported content is reviewed.

As for minors, Whoop’s minimum age is 13 for all members (higher in some countries), meaning customers of that age can share their data subject to consents and privacy settings. Asked whether a parent could follow a teenager’s data, he didn’t answer directly and pointed to the privacy controls.

Ahmed also said Whoop doesn’t sell member data. “Our product is not you; it’s the membership,” he said, adding that the company doesn’t license data to third parties.

He was candid about dealmaking. Asked about Oura’s announcement this week that it will pay [$625 million for Counsel Health](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/oura-to-acquire-ai-native-primary-care-startup-counsel-health-for-625m/), a service offering on-demand physicians, Ahmed said he expects “more consolidation” among AI-focused, clinician-focused, and wearable companies, and that Whoop is always on the prowl. Its deep relationship with members, he said, puts it “in a position to be an acquirer.”

He was more guarded about the company’s own finances, declining to share revenue or say whether Whoop is profitable. He did say that the company now has about 1,200 employees, membership on its software platform has increased more than 70% a year for three years running, and that it expects to have nearly 4 million members by year-end.

As for an IPO (the company raised a [very big round](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/whoop-valuation-10b-series-g-fundraise/) earlier this year), he said only that Whoop “will certainly IPO in the future.” He also wouldn’t comment on Oura, which shelved its own IPO of up to $2.2 billion in September citing market uncertainty.

In the meantime, Whoop is widening its reach. The company announced that its devices will be sold through Ulta stores beginning next March (around 500 locations of the beauty retailer to start, and a target to retail in more than a thousand stores eventually). Indeed, Ahmed said Whoop’s female audience has more than doubled from last year.

As for competition, in addition to Google’s new [Fitbit Air band,](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/google-unveils-whoop-like-screenless-fitbit-air/) Whoop may soon have to contend with Apple, which is reportedly developing a [Whoop-style band](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/apple-is-developing-new-fitness-tracker-aimed-at-rivaling-whoop). But Ahmed mostly shrugged off those concerns, suggesting that building a product doesn’t guarantee success. Instead, he said, Whoop’s job is to stay true to the “aspiring” people it serves.

## Summary

**TLDR:** Whoop Humans could turn health data into meaningful support, but social engagement is a dangerous incentive when vulnerability becomes the content.

- Whoop Humans lets members follow one another and selectively share sleep, recovery, workouts, achievements, photos, and commentary.
- The feature is opt-in, available at every membership tier without an additional charge, and allows users to control followers and shared data.
- Unlike the existing Teams feature, Humans uses individual relationships rather than group-wide visibility and leaderboards.
- Beta users engaged more with low recovery scores than near-perfect ones, suggesting the network may help friends, relatives, and trainers notice when someone is struggling.
- The launch supports Whoop’s expansion into medical wellness alongside ECG, atrial-fibrillation detection, blood-pressure insights, blood tests, and medical-record uploads.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** Whoop Humans 可能讓健康數據帶來真正關懷，但當低潮與脆弱成為社群內容，追求互動就會變成危險誘因。

- Whoop Humans 讓會員互相追蹤，並自行選擇是否分享睡眠、恢復、運動、成就、照片與文字。
- 功能全面採自願加入，各種會員方案都能免費使用，使用者也可決定誰能追蹤以及能看到哪些資料。
- 既有 Teams 以群組和排行榜為核心，Humans 則採一對一關係與細緻權限，不會讓同一群組所有人自動互看資訊。
- 測試期間，低恢復分數獲得的互動比接近滿分的數據更多，顯示親友或教練可能藉此察覺使用者正處於低潮。
- 這項社群功能也延續 Whoop 進軍醫療健康的路線，與心電圖、心房顫動偵測、血壓洞察、驗血及病歷上傳相互呼應。
