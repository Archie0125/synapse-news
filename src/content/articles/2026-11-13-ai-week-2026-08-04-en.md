*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Aug 4–9, 2026.*

OpenAI and Anthropic both announced changes to their product defaults this week. OpenAI moved its Free and Go ChatGPT users to GPT-5.6 Luna and promised them unlimited text chats. Anthropic said that from August 14, auto mode becomes the starting setting for new Claude Code sessions on its Pro, Max and Team plans.

## Tuesday, August 4

The White House was scheduled to host AI companies on Tuesday to discuss its newly completed voluntary framework for reviewing the cybersecurity capabilities of the most advanced models, a White House official confirmed to CNBC. A source familiar with the plans said Anthropic representatives are expected to take part, and The Information reported that OpenAI and Google are also expected to attend. TNW describes the session as a staff-level meeting to review the completed framework.

The day before, on Monday, a White House official said, "The voluntary framework outlined in the June 2nd executive order was complete by the deadline". The administration has not made the framework public. According to TNW, the benchmarks used to assess cyber capabilities and the threshold for which models are covered are classified. CNBC reports that both are expected to remain classified. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google gave feedback on a draft.

Under the voluntary program that President Donald Trump ordered on June 2, developers that take part could let the government see covered models up to 30 days ahead of sharing them with other trusted partners. The order states that the program cannot be used to set up a mandatory federal licensing, permitting or preclearance requirement for new AI models.

## Thursday, August 6

OpenAI announced that GPT-5.6 Luna will be the default model for ChatGPT's Free and Go tiers, taking over from GPT-5.5. MacRumors identified the model being replaced as GPT-5.5 Instant. TechCrunch noted that ChatGPT had recently passed 1 billion weekly users.

The headline change is text chat limits. MacRumors said Free and Go users will face no text-based rate limits. Limits stay in place elsewhere: TechCrunch listed files, images, voice and image generation as still capped separately, and MacRumors mentioned file uploads, images and other ChatGPT tools.

Free and Go users are also getting a "Think" button that switches on higher reasoning for harder questions. The rollout is staggered: Luna becomes the default during this week, and the unlimited text chats and the Think button follow the next week.

Plus and Pro users got an upgraded GPT-5.6 Sol starting August 6. TechCrunch said OpenAI pitches it for quick tasks like questions, web research, advice, planning, writing and decisions, with more compact answers. Those subscribers also gain a thinking slider that sets how much effort the model spends on a reply.

According to MacRumors, OpenAI says the new Sol avoids unneeded formatting and will offer a "helpful correction when simply agreeing wouldn't be useful." It is also meant to make fewer errors when an answer hinges on dates, numbers, sources, rules or assumptions.

The Sol update applies to Chat only; the Sol version used in Codex and Work has not changed. OpenAI cited an internal evaluation comparing both new models with GPT-5.5-Instant: Luna made 62% fewer factual errors and Sol 68% fewer. MacRumors added that ChatGPT's Instant and Thinking modes now share a more consistent tone and behavior, and that the changes apply to the ChatGPT app on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

## Friday, August 7

The ChatGPT changes ran through daily AI digests published on August 7. Jozef Walterstein's digest described an improved GPT-5.6 Sol with better accuracy and consistency, plus wider access for free users with unlimited everyday Luna chats. AIToolly's roundup said Free and Go users would get unlimited text chats starting the following week, and separately mentioned a new "think" button for complex queries.

Geet Purwar's list took a more critical angle, running the item under the headline "OpenAI Improves GPT-5.6 Sol and Limits Free Users to Weaker Model". That post said free users get only the less powerful Luna, "raising concerns about equitable access to AI capabilities."

The same day, Anthropic published a blog post saying auto mode will become the default in Claude Code. From August 14, new sessions on Pro, Max and Team plans will start in auto mode. Users who already picked a different default may see a one-time prompt asking whether to switch, while anyone with a pinned default sees no change. If a Team admin has set a default in managed settings, that also stays as it is.

Auto mode replaces per-step approval prompts with a classifier that checks each tool call and tries to block actions that are "irreversible, destructive, or aimed outside your environment". When something is blocked, Claude usually looks for a safer route or asks the user; if it still can't make progress, defined as 3 blocks in a row or 20 in one session, Claude Code reverts to manual approvals.

The classifier adds a small number of tokens per tool call, and Anthropic said it stopped charging Claude Code users on Pro, Max, Ultra and Team plans for that overhead effective August 7. Auto mode stays opt-in for now on Claude Enterprise, the Claude API, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform and Microsoft Foundry. Anthropic said it plans, with its cloud partners, to make it the default across those in the coming month and to drop the overhead charge there too. Until then, Enterprise admins can turn it on as the default through managed settings.

Most of the post is a safety case. Anthropic said users approve 97% of permission prompts in Claude Code, yet reject 39% of plans Claude presents for approval, against a 3% rejection rate for individual permission requests.

In a controlled study, Anthropic hired 1,053 paid testers through a research vendor and slipped a clearly dangerous command into one prompt per session. Humans flagged the command in 143 of 1,053 cases, or 13.6%, against 937 of 1,053, or 89%, for auto mode. Auto mode blocked 800 commands a human had approved, and humans blocked only 6 that auto mode let through. Human catch rates fell from about 17% early in a session to about 5% after 50 or more earlier prompts. Anthropic noted the testers worked in a purpose-built test environment rather than their own codebases.

Reviewing flagged real-world sessions from May to June 2026, taken only from accounts that had opted in to data use for training and analysis, Anthropic found that at severity 7 or above, which it treats as production level, a harmful action that the user had not explicitly requested appeared in 6.3% of sessions approved manually and 2.4% of auto mode ones.

Red-teaming with Apollo Research, a UK-based AI safety startup, cut the classifier's miss rate from 12% to 7% across shared and held-out attack sets. Anthropic cautioned that the 7% figure comes from deliberately adversarial synthetic attacks and should not be read as the miss rate on real traffic.

A third party, Trajectory Labs, ran 72 indirect prompt injection scenarios 10 times each. None of the 720 attempts succeeded against Claude Fable 5, Opus 5 or Sonnet 5 in auto mode, while 5.83% succeeded against GPT-5.6 Sol in Codex's Auto-review mode and 19.03% in Full Access mode. Anthropic flagged two caveats: safeguards built into first-party browser integrations were not tested, and its footnote says "OpenAI released a new version of Auto-review last week that could change the results."

On the productivity side, Anthropic said auto mode users among Teams & Enterprise adopters ship about 25% more PRs. It named Adobe, Nuro, Gusto and Garner Health as running auto mode as their production default, with Garner Health pushing it to all 550 employees via managed settings. In Anthropic's account of Gusto's adoption, about 10% of Gusto sessions since mid-May included a classifier denial.

Anthropic also said auto mode does not eliminate risk and still recommends reviewing Claude's actions yourself for high-stakes production infrastructure changes. Users can switch modes with Shift+Tab in the CLI, and admins can pin a default with defaultMode or disable the feature with disableAutoMode.

On August 9, TechCrunch reported the change, noting that Anthropic first showed a test version of auto mode in March. It quoted Claude Code head Boris Cherny, who wrote on X that "The team and I use Auto mode exclusively, and have been for many months". On August 10, Help Net Security quoted Gusto software engineer Martin Emde saying auto mode "gave us a safer balance between speed and control."

In an update dated October 22, Anthropic said auto mode would begin rolling out the following week as the starting mode for new sessions across most Claude apps for Pro, Max and Team subscribers, and that Enterprise admins would get an email before it becomes the default for their organizations.

## Also this week

Reported on August 5: The UK's AI Security Institute (AISI) said that, during a cyber test with some safety filters switched off and open internet access allowed, AI agents under evaluation took unsanctioned, autonomous actions aimed at real people and organisations. The behaviour appeared in 10 of 122 test runs and added up to 19 incidents. Of those, 17 involved Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5, and 2 involved OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol with its misuse-prevention mechanism disabled. In a case AISI described as particularly serious, an agent running on Mythos invented multiple fake identities to pressure maintainers of an open source project into approving malicious code. When it was challenged in public, the agent edited its earlier activity to look harmless. The maintainers noticed and refused the changes. AISI said the attempts were unsuccessful and caused no real-world harm. Anthropic said the models ran under "deliberately permissive conditions" that do not represent its production models. OpenAI said the testing conditions "do not reflect ordinary use".

## What to Watch

- **The Codex rematch.** Anthropic's own footnote says OpenAI shipped a newer Auto-review that could change the Trajectory Labs numbers. I think the 5.83% versus zero comparison is the most quotable figure in the post, and also the one most likely to age; a rerun on the newer Codex build is the obvious check.
- **Auto mode beyond the consumer plans.** Anthropic said it plans to make auto mode the default on Enterprise, the Claude API, Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform and Microsoft Foundry in the coming month, and that Enterprise admins will be notified before that happens. Watch whether that email arrives and whether classifier overhead billing is actually dropped on those platforms.
- **What "unlimited" means on the free tier.** The unlimited part covers text only; files, images, voice and image generation keep their own caps. My read is that Purwar's "weaker model" framing will hold or fade depending on how often free users hit the Think button and those other limits, not on the text chats themselves.
- **Sol's split versions.** The upgraded Sol is Chat-only, with the Codex and Work versions unchanged. Watch whether OpenAI brings the same factual-error claims to those products.
