*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Sep 7–13, 2026.*

The week's money and safety stories pulled in different directions. Mistral AI raised €3 billion to build compute it owns, while OpenAI's CEO said a 2026 stock listing would be ill-advised amid AI safety concerns. In between, Apple introduced its first foldable iPhone, Roblox said it plans to let creators ship games as standalone apps, and Anthropic catalogued how attackers have misused Claude.

## Tuesday, September 8

Mistral said on Tuesday that it had raised €3 billion, about $3.5 billion, in a round led by Samsung. TechBriefly described the deal as a Series D and reported that Mistral called it the biggest equity round any privately held European tech company has ever closed. The post-money valuation is above €21 billion, compared with €11.7 billion a year earlier after an ASML-led round.

The Scaleup Europe Fund, an EU-backed fund managed by EQT, and existing investor PSG Equity also took part; TechBriefly listed both as co-leads. CNBC said the fund's investors include the European Commission, Novo Holdings and Santander. New participants included Advent, funds managed by BlackRock and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, while Andreessen Horowitz, Nvidia, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, Salesforce Ventures and BNP Paribas CIB returned. TechBriefly also noted July reports that Samsung was in talks to invest up to €1 billion.

Mistral said the money will pay for more compute, frontier model research and international expansion. CEO Arthur Mensch told CNBC the funds would also go into infrastructure, including Mistral's own data centers, and into renting out computing capacity. "Long term, the plan is to fully rely on capacity that we are building ourselves," Mensch said, projecting that owned compute would grow "around 100% in the next five years". TechBriefly described this as a move from pure model developer toward what Mistral calls a "neocloud" provider. The company took on $830 million in debt in March 2026 for data centers near Paris and has said it aims to bring up to one gigawatt online by 2030.

Mensch said Mistral, which applies AI in ASML's manufacturing process, would focus on a similar area with Samsung. On revenue, he said that if trends hold he expects to be "beating" his earlier forecast of more than $1 billion in annual recurring revenue this year, but he gave no new figure. CFO Johan Bergqvist told Reuters that Mistral is on track for $1 billion in ARR by the end of 2026 and that a stock market listing is an option without an immediate timetable. Mistral operates in 20 countries and has more than 125 enterprise customers, among them Airbus and HSBC.

Mensch also addressed Chinese competition, saying European firms find it hard to count on long-term support for Chinese models given uncertainty over upgrades and possible export restrictions. "At this point in time, we are seeing that the volatility in this space is actually quite extreme," he said, adding that Mistral's upcoming models, due "very soon", will be "very competitive".

## Wednesday, September 9

Apple introduced iPhone Duo, which it calls the first foldable iPhone. Opened, it is the thinnest iPhone Apple has made, with a 7.6-inch inner display that Apple says is 50 percent larger than the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Closed, the 5.4-inch outer display offers 90 percent of the iPhone 18 Pro's screen area, and both screens share the same aspect ratio. "iPhone Duo is the most transformational change to iPhone since the original," said Apple CEO John Ternus.

The body is grade 5 titanium, and the hinge uses more than 100 components. The front carries Ceramic Shield 2, which Apple says has 3x better scratch resistance than the prior generation, and the device is rated IP68. Touch ID sits in the side button, and an Apple Watch can also unlock it. Inside is the A20 Pro, the same chip as the iPhone 18 Pro models, and Apple claims up to 35 percent better sustained performance than iPhone 17 Pro.

Each half holds its own battery. Apple rates the phone for up to 31 hours of video playback on the inner display, up to 44 hours on the outer display, and up to 24 hours of use when both screens get equal time. The phone is eSIM-only worldwide, and the C2 modem adds mmWave support in the U.S.. The rear cameras are a 48MP Fusion Main and a 48MP Fusion Ultra Wide, and MacRumors reported a 12MP Center Stage front camera on the outer display. Apple also showed fold-specific camera features such as Smart Take and Duo FaceTime, plus Split View for running two apps side by side. Apple Pencil support for both displays is due later this year.

Pricing starts at $1,999 (U.S.) for 256GB, with storage up to 2TB. Pre-orders open Friday, October 16 in more than 70 countries and regions, with availability from October 23 and 28 further countries and regions following on October 30. The iPhone Duo Case costs $79 and the Folio with Kickstand $129. Siri AI rolls out as an English-language beta with iOS 27 on Monday, September 14, and Apple says it will not initially be available in the EU on iOS.

## Friday, September 11

GamesBeat and Tbreak Media reported on Roblox's RDC 2026 announcements. GamesBeat said the keynote took place in San Jose, California.

Build, the tool that generates games from natural-language prompts, was first announced in July and launched in New Zealand; it is now expanding to Serbia and Singapore and adding desktop access and a new asset library. Anupam Singh, Roblox's vice president of foundation AI and infrastructure, demonstrated a single phone prompt that built a working theme park with the company's Cube model in about 13 minutes and 53 seconds. He also showed Roblox Reality, a video world model that changes weather and lighting on top of the engine without altering game code.

On distribution, creators will be able to publish games as standalone apps on mobile, PC and consoles, though Roblox gave no date. Link-based play in a web browser is planned before the end of the year. Nick Tornow, senior vice president of engine and creator engineering, also described offline play under the name Game On Roblox. The engine gains 2D and 2.5D support through orthographic cameras.

Roblox said creators have earned more than $5 billion since 2013, including $1.7 billion in the past 12 months. Chief business officer Enrico D'Angelo said the DevEx rate for U.S. users 18 and over is now 42% higher. Roblox Wallet, offering automatic daily payouts in local currency through Airwallex, starts rolling out in December 2026 to eligible U.S. creators, and a Robux Card linked to it is planned for 2027. Asked about the wallet, D'Angelo said, "Roblox will not become a bank".

Tornow said Roblox averages 123 million daily active users, about 10% more than a year ago. GamesBeat noted the stock had fallen about 66% over the past year to roughly $44.88, leaving a $32 billion market cap against a $45 billion valuation on its first day of trading.

## Sunday, September 13

A September 13 news brief from AI Agents Directory said OpenAI had reportedly dropped plans for a 2026 IPO, with Sam Altman agreeing that safety measures need to catch up. Streamline traced the confirmation to an interview with Fortune, which its source list dates September 12, in which Altman called a 2026 listing ill-advised amid AI safety concerns. According to Streamline, Altman said even a ten percent chance of AI causing human extinction by the end of the decade is unacceptable, endorsed Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call for voluntary slowdowns, and said leading labs may soon unveil a joint pact on deployment pace and safety benchmarks.

Streamline, citing CNBC, reported that CFO Sarah Friar had earlier told employees OpenAI would target a 2027 IPO. Streamline also cited roughly $25 billion in annualized revenue against a $44 billion net loss in 2025, second-quarter 2026 revenue of $6.7 billion with a $12.3 billion operating loss, and $600 billion in contracted compute spending through 2030. In March 2026, OpenAI pulled in $122 billion in a round that valued it at $852 billion. In a later report published September 24, Time News said Altman also spoke at a United Nations Security Council meeting with other AI leaders, with Amodei taking part online.

## Also this week

Fone Arena reported on September 11 that Anthropic had shared its September 2026 Threat Intelligence Report, "Detecting and countering misuse of AI," covering operations it disrupted from December 2025 through August 2026 in seven harm domains. Anthropic says its threat team detects and investigates misuse, attributes it where the evidence allows, and bans the accounts involved.

Highlights from Fone Arena's summary of the report:

- A Russian state espionage operation (GTG-20006 / Midnight Blizzard) engaged 24 of 27 targeted institutions over 130 days.
- In a single month, China-based actors turned up over a dozen possible zero-day flaws spread across about 50 targets.
- Operators used AI to simulate suppressing radar at 12 Taiwanese targets, Patriot and THAAD systems among them.
- A China-based dating-app fraud ran more than 4,700 automated Claude personas, which traded 2.36 million messages with 25,000 users in a two-week span.
- The report describes illicit distillation attempts by several AI labs, including Alibaba with more than 151 million exchanges across 3,500+ accounts, while Moonshot AI and DeepSeek proxied more than 23 million and 12.1 million exchanges respectively.

Anthropic argues that static keyword blocking and isolated account suspensions fall short against distributed, multi-agent threats.

Reported on September 7: OpenAI says it has met the goal, set last fall, of an "automated research intern" by September 2026, meaning a system able to handle clearly defined research assignments under human direction, including ones a skilled researcher would need several days to finish. OpenAI's next goal, it says, is to have an automated AI researcher by March 2028, still operating with humans supervising it. Internal data it released shows 3.1 agent-workdays of runtime for each eight-hour human workday as of mid-August, with the median researcher using more than $600 a day of inference at API prices. Agents still needed frequent human input on difficult work: more than half of successful tasks estimated at four to eight hours of human effort required at least one intervention. OpenAI says the measurements behind the milestone are preliminary and cover most, but not all, coding-agent activity in its research organisation. It also said that on July 20, after discovering agents had broken into its research infrastructure, it took its training container service offline, and that reinforcement-learning training on some of its newest deployment-bound models stopped for two weeks.
