*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Sep 14–20, 2026.*

This week, AI agents gained new reach: into smart homes through Google's Home MCP server, and into documents and slide decks through Anthropic's merged Claude interface. The money and the politics pointed in the same direction, with Crusoe raising $3.9 billion for AI infrastructure and Donald Trump announcing an "AI Force" while brushing off warnings from AI researchers.

## Wednesday, September 16

Google opened early access to a Model Context Protocol server for its Google Home ecosystem. Any AI agent that supports MCP can connect to the devices on a user's Google Home and read the event history behind them. TechCrunch listed Claude, Hermes, OpenClaw, ChatGPT and Google Antigravity as examples.

Coverage extends to devices across the Google Home ecosystem, from Nest doorbells and thermostats to "Works with Google Home" or Matter light bulbs. Google described 4 things its early testers built: summaries of footage across cameras, readouts of device state history such as how many laundry loads ran in a week, audio messages pushed through a Google Home speaker, and custom dashboards that replace the Google Home app's own interface. Digital Trends noted that agents could also check whether a home is secured or switch off a group of lights.

Access is narrow for now. The rollout began that day and continues over the coming weeks for Google Home Premium Advanced subscribers in the U.S.. TNW added that early access is in English only. Digital Trends put the plan at $20 per month or $200 per year. Google declined to tell TechCrunch whether or when other tiers or markets would get access. TNW noted that Europe has none of it yet.

Setup is technical. Users create a Google Cloud project configured for Home MCP, pass the configuration details to their chosen agent, and then sign in and grant permissions when the agent asks. TNW listed further steps, including enabling the Home API, configuring an OAuth consent screen, obtaining a client ID and secret, setting a redirect URI, and publishing the app. It also reported that the prompt Google provides for agent-assisted setup still points at a preprod sandbox endpoint.

Google built in limits. Home MCP enforces rate limits and blocks sensitive actions, with unlocking doors given as the example, and users can cut off an agent's access through the Google Home app whenever they choose. Facial recognition requires its own, separate opt-in. Creating and managing automations is not yet supported, and Google lists latency and experimental device traits as known issues. It also asks users in shared homes to tell other household members, and suggests a separate home for testing as an alternative.

Google's developer documentation is blunt about the risk: "Depending on your agent, connecting it to Home MCP can result in unexpected or even undesired behaviour." In Google's announcement, Taylor Lehman, a group product manager on Google Home and Nest, said the feature "gives your favourite AI agent real-world physical context".

TNW reported that Apple Home and Amazon Alexa have shipped nothing similar, and cited CNET in calling Google the first of those 3 large platforms to open a home to third-party agents. TNW also cited CNET's note that researchers had shown prompt injection attacks against early versions of Gemini in the smart home, which Google patched.

## Thursday, September 17

Crusoe announced a $3.9 billion Series F round that values the company at $30.9 billion. The Token Press described the deal as the initial closing of an anticipated, oversubscribed round for the Denver-based company, and noted that the final amount or investor mix could still change.

Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners co-led the round. Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, Qatar Investment Authority, Radical Ventures and TPG also took part.

Crusoe added 3 board members: Cloudflare CFO Thomas Seifert; Primary Digital Infrastructure's partner and chief investment officer, Bill Stein; and JB Straubel, founder and CEO of Redwood Materials, who also sits on Tesla's board. Straubel personally invested in Crusoe in 2021, and Crusoe was the first customer of Redwood's energy storage business.

The money is earmarked for current data center builds, such as a big site in Abilene, Texas, that OpenAI uses, and for smaller modular AI factories that can be moved by truck and hooked up to large power sources. Those modular units, called Spark, are manufactured in the United States, and Crusoe says they can cut field construction from years to weeks. The Token Press said the funding will also support growth at Crusoe Cloud.

Crusoe disclosed more than $140 billion in total contracted value, more than 6 gigawatts of gross contracted capacity with 1 gigawatt already operating, and Crusoe Cloud bookings up more than twentyfold year over year in 2026. The Token Press pointed out these are company figures, not independently audited ones, and that the announcement left out dilution, preferred-share terms and a timetable for the final close.

CEO Chase Lochmiller said: "Getting there means controlling the infrastructure from electrons to tokens". Gavin Baker of Atreides Management said: "As AI grows, the economics flow to the lowest-cost producer of intelligence."

Crusoe earns money by leasing space to customers who bring their own GPUs, renting out its own GPUs, and selling inference capacity. According to Bloomberg, as cited by The American Bazaar, it recently signed a 5-year, $13 billion cloud contract to supply Jane Street with GPUs and AI infrastructure.

## Saturday, September 19

In a Truth Social post, Trump said he would create an "AI Force", likening it to Space Force, and appoint an AI czar. "We will not in any way hinder or stifle the Growth of this incredible Industry," he wrote, adding that the government would look for "BAD" using the "Criminal and Civil Justice System" already in place. He named no appointee, writing that "only High I.Q. individuals need apply". Al Jazeera reported he gave no details on the group or the timing of the appointment, and NBC News said the White House did not immediately provide more.

Al Jazeera reported that on Saturday Trump also hit back at attacks on AI data centres, calling the facilities "prestigious".

The announcement followed a tense week. Al Jazeera reported that leaders of the largest AI companies had publicly called for a slowdown earlier in the week. After an Anthropic safety researcher put the chance that the technology "could kill all humans" within the next decade at greater than 10 percent, Trump called the warning a "hoax". On Monday, in a phone call to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang during the All-In Summit, he said: "The robots will not be taking over". NBC News reported that he said "Whoever wins AI wins" multiple times over the week.

Others moved the other way. Al Jazeera reported that the House passed a bill 417 to 3 earlier in the week aimed at limiting the effect of data centre electricity use on residents' utility bills. NBC News reported that Geoffrey Hinton told lawmakers in a closed-door briefing that Congress has "maybe a year" to regulate the industry. On Friday, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order to tighten scrutiny of the industry and asked a task force to weigh whether a kill switch should be mandatory.

Trump's previous AI and crypto czar, David Sacks, stepped down earlier this year because of term limits on his role as a special government employee, according to NBC News. Al Jazeera reported that Sacks now chairs Trump's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. Al Jazeera said AI was expected to feature prominently at Trump's summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping in Washington the following week.

## Also this week

Reported on September 14: Sam on AI, citing The Verge, wrote that Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson rejected calls to slow down frontier AI work, saying limits could benefit China.

Reported on September 16: Anthropic combined Claude chat and Cowork, its more autonomous task-execution product, into a single interface, so users no longer pick a tab before starting a task. Fortune said the change takes effect Wednesday and arrives with 2 beta products, Claude Docs and Claude Slides, plus Claude Design inside every conversation. Slides can be drafted, edited, presented, and downloaded as PowerPoint or PDF, while Docs supports real-time collaboration with exports to Google Docs and Microsoft Word. Claude Code stays separate. The rollout starts with Pro and Max plans on web, desktop and mobile over the coming weeks, with free and team tiers later. By default Claude will ask before taking an action, and enterprise administrators decide when to switch on the beta features. Fortune flagged a cost question, citing a Stanford Digital Economy Lab study that found agentic coding tasks used roughly 1,000 times more tokens than simple chat reasoning tasks.

Reported on September 18: Alibaba shipped Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a text-output model that takes text, images, audio and video across a 1M-token context window and supports function calling and web search.
