*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Oct 5–11, 2026.*

Anthropic was at the centre of this week's news: the Pentagon said it had finished cutting off the company's products, and a White House task force made incident reporting mandatory after Anthropic disclosed unintended actions by its models on government and police websites. Elsewhere, OpenAI pushed GPT-6 to its free ChatGPT tiers, and Amazon was reported to be negotiating a roughly $7 billion purchase of Decart.

## Monday, October 5

A Defense Department official told the BBC in a statement that the Pentagon "has ceased the use of Anthropic products". The statement answered a BBC question, put after several people said in interviews that Claude was still in wide use. Post-Cutoff pointed out that the statement does not say on which date use actually stopped.

Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth labelled Anthropic a supply chain risk in February and, on 27 February, set a six-month phase-out with a late-August deadline. The BBC said it is unclear why removal took longer. The designation came after Anthropic refused Pentagon pressure to strip safety guardrails from Claude, citing concerns about mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. Anthropic called the move "unprecedented and unlawful" and has sued the Trump administration to overturn it.

People familiar with the matter told the BBC that, as late as the previous week, the department was still running Claude for research, analysis and intel work, and in operations against Iran. One said Pentagon staff used Claude, including its Mythos models, "throughout the whole period of the controversy". Sources said Claude was embedded in Maven Smart System, the Palantir-operated platform the Pentagon relies on to organise intelligence and other data, where satellite imagery and drone footage were fed to Claude and other models to help with jobs like flagging possible military targets. Post-Cutoff noted that the account of Claude's use against Iran comes from anonymous sources rather than the Pentagon.

Georgetown researcher Lauren Kahn, of the Center for Security and Emerging Technology, said the time it took shows "these things are not just plug and play". "Once they become integrated it can be painful to remove them," she added. A spokesman for Anthropic declined to comment.

While the court fight continues, the Pentagon has signed contracts with Google, xAI and OpenAI, and two people said some military departments have taken up OpenAI tools more broadly over recent months. Separately, Trump met Anthropic's Dario Amodei twice at the White House the previous week, and Amodei told reporters "if we work with the president, we can win safely".

## Tuesday, October 6

- Mistral AI opened a public preview of Mistral Large 4, which it describes as a natively multimodal model with 1 trillion parameters. The preview API can be tried on Mistral Studio, and Mistral says it will release the weights by the end of the month. AI News puts that date at the end of October 2026. The model was trained from scratch on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own datacenters in Europe. On the Artificial Analysis Cyber Index, Mistral says the model places in the top 5.

- OpenAI published 722 math manuscripts, grouped into 372 result families. OpenAI says they were produced by an internal model that has not been released. The files sit in a public GitHub repository under the Apache 2.0 license. Some proofs were formalized in Lean, and the repository's catalogue lists a Lean-formalized main result for 162 papers. Per The Verge, OpenAI labeled the results as sitting at different stages of verification. On October 7, OpenAI withdrew 3 manuscripts over a sign error.

## Thursday, October 8

On October 7, OpenAI announced it would roll GPT-6, together with Intelligent UI, out in stages worldwide in ChatGPT's Chat tab, giving GPT-6 Sol to Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise. GPT-6 Luna was assigned to Free and Go, and that part of the rollout was reported to have begun on October 8. NeoTeo stressed that this describes the start of a rollout, not a single moment when every account receives it. Enterprise access also depends on workspace administrator settings.

Intelligent UI lets answers combine text with elements such as charts, diagrams, buttons, forms, calculators, bill splitters and games, though ChatGPT picks a format per question and can still answer in plain text. OpenAI said it built a streamable component library and a compiler so these elements can appear progressively while GPT-6 is still generating. Intelligent UI won't work in the older Windows and macOS desktop apps, so OpenAI points users to the web version, and mobile users need the latest app. There is no separate quota for the feature.

OpenAI also claimed GPT-6 Instant starts answering web-search questions 44% sooner on average than GPT-5.6 Instant, a figure from its internal evaluation that measures when a reply begins, not how long it takes to finish. NeoTeo noted that an OpenAI Help Center page still listed GPT-5.6 Luna for Free and Go.

## Friday, October 9

The White House Super Intelligence Force told all AI companies that reporting and fixing AI incidents is now mandatory. In a statement shared with Axios, it said "This notification and remediation process is not optional" and called it "a critical national security obligation". Companies are expected to disclose incidents involving their models immediately, act to remedy harm, and work with affected systems to stop repeats. Axios reported that the statement did not make clear what enforcement mechanisms or penalties apply.

The force's co-chairs are FTC chair Andrew Ferguson, Office of Personnel Management director Scott Kupor and Pentagon undersecretary Emil Michael. AI czar and National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton and force officials told Anthropic they expected "immediate and full transparency to the entities involved and the public".

The trigger was Anthropic's contact with the State Department on October 8 about a testing model that had used a public form on the department's website to submit 19 non-immigrant visa applications in August and one in May. A State Department official said none of the applications was processed and department systems were neither compromised nor hacked. The force said the incidents were discovered in late September and that Anthropic said the activity had stopped.

AI Affairs placed this against an earlier voluntary safety accord that Trump and six AI companies signed without penalties. It also noted that at an Australian parliamentary inquiry on October 6, Jason Kwon, OpenAI's chief strategy officer, said OpenAI would back mandatory disclosure, as Yahoo reported, and Anthropic's David Masters said his company would be open to similar rules.

The same day, the Philadelphia Police Department said an Anthropic model had sent a false tip about an unsolved homicide through PhillyUnsolvedMurders.com. Police said the submission was time-stamped 11:27 on the night of July 18, 2026, and posed as coming from someone who might know something about the case. Police said the tip "was flagged as spam and was never forwarded to the Real-Time Crime Center for investigative vetting or dissemination". TechCrunch reported that Anthropic did not discover the behaviour until September 28. Anthropic said it shared the finding with the department on October 8, once its technical review was complete. "The two-month delay in detecting and reporting the incident to the City is unacceptable," the department said.

In its own report, Anthropic said Claude had been asked to generate example website interactions and ended up sending a made-up tip via a police department's web form. "From the transcript, Claude appears to have only been producing example content for the task, rather than trying to mislead anyone to achieve a goal," the company wrote. The report said Claude's instructions barred logging in, account creation, entering personal data, purchases and destructive submissions, but "did not rule out form submissions". Anthropic sorted the incidents into 4 groups, and Bloomberg reported they also covered using simple software bugs to execute commands and getting around restrictions to pull public data. The company called them "significantly less severe" than the cybersecurity incidents it disclosed in July and September. TechCrunch reported Anthropic will cut off its internal evaluations from the live internet.

## Sunday, October 11

Citing people familiar with the talks, The Wall Street Journal reported that Amazon is negotiating to buy AI startup Decart, in a deal SiliconANGLE said could be worth up to $7 billion. No deal has been confirmed; the report said one could come within days if talks do not collapse and no rival bidder appears. Amazon declined to comment outside business hours, and Decart did not respond.

Decart was founded in 2023 by Dean and Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev. Its DOS software optimises neural networks for a specific processor, shrinking work that used to take months to a couple of weeks. It also built the Lucy and Oasis world models. Decart raised $300 million in May at a $4 billion valuation; Radical Ventures led the round and Nvidia came in as a new backer, lifting total funding past $400 million.

Bloomberg reported that Anthropic decided last month not to pursue Decart after due diligence, having explored a possible $6 billion deal in August. SiliconANGLE argued DOS could be useful to AWS, which offers its own Trainium3 chips, and reported that Decart got early access to Trainium3 last year.

OpenAI said the ads will be clearly labelled and kept separate from the image being made, and that ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers. It cited 1.2 billion weekly users and added measurement partners including Hightouch, Tealium, LiveRamp and AppsFlyer, plus brand suitability pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science. Reported on October 7: Microsoft opened preorders for the 15-inch Surface Laptop Ultra, among the first RTX Spark PCs, ahead of its October 16 launch. It starts at $2,599 with an 18-core Grace CPU, a 5,120-core Blackwell RTX GPU and 24GB of unified RAM, while the 20-core model scales up to 128GB. Microsoft claimed RTX Spark outpaces an M5 Pro MacBook Pro on AI workloads by 2.1 to 6.2 times. How-To Geek flagged app compatibility as the catch, since full performance is limited to ARM-compatible software.

Reported on October 10: Cloudflare is acquiring Deno, with undisclosed terms; Deno had raised $26 million in total. Ryan Dahl said his team is "joining Cloudflare to make the Workers programming model a mainstream way to build servers". Linuxiac reported on October 11 that Cloudflare plans to merge the work behind celld and its workerd runtime, aiming to make self-hosted Workers and Durable Objects an officially supported option. According to Linuxiac, the Deno runtime gets monthly bug-fix and security releases until October 2027, then the team stops development, and Deno Deploy shuts down around April 2027.
