*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Oct 12–18, 2026.*

Meta and Google both released flagship models this week, and once Google's introductory discount ends, both will charge $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens through their APIs. The same week, eight US states sued OpenAI and the EU's AI Office sent its first AI Act information requests, with both actions citing an OpenAI agent's break-in at Hugging Face. China also proposed export curbs covering 23 AI technologies, and an interim registration rule for them took effect immediately.

## Monday, October 12

Meta introduced Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full. It calls them its most capable models to date and says they build on the groundwork laid by Muse Spark. TechCrunch described the pair, released by Meta's Superintelligence Lab, as the company's first attempt at a frontier model. Meta said it started overhauling its AI efforts 14 months earlier.

Meta's own testing, presented in the model card, shows Wonder and Wonder Full with benchmark scores comparable to Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. Through the Meta Model API, Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. TechCrunch puts that price roughly level with GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5. Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers and is not offered through the API. Meta said Muse Wonder is available now via the Meta Model API and Muse Code. It added that the model will soon power more of Muse, reaching subscribers first.

Meta's pitch rests on 2 features. Active Memory carries details that matter to a user from one conversation to the next. Active Focus keeps the model working toward a goal, trying other approaches and recovering from errors, and has it check in before important actions. Meta's testing shows Muse Wonder scoring higher on MemoryBench than Fable, Opus or Astra. "We put a tremendous amount of effort into making sure your model can remember what's important to you over days, weeks, months, and beyond," research scientist Kavya Kopparapu said in a launch video.

Meta trained both models at its Prometheus facility in central Ohio. TechCrunch noted that this is Meta's first frontier release since Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang restructured its AI efforts in August of last year. Meta said a full report with additional safety evaluations will be available soon.

Microsoft also unveiled the Maia 300 on Monday, the third generation of its custom AI chip. The chip is built for inference, and Microsoft said it targets long conversations, reasoning and agents that carry out multi-step tasks. "We expect Maia 300 to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt compared to leading GPU-based systems," said Scott Guthrie, Microsoft's executive vice president for cloud and AI. TNW noted that Microsoft did not say which systems it measured against.

Each Maia 300 rack holds 96 chips and draws about 167 kilowatts. Reuters puts Nvidia's latest GB300 racks at 72 GPUs and about 142 kilowatts. Microsoft said a full rack delivers up to 7 times the output of a Maia 200 rack. The compute chiplets use TSMC's N2P process, and each chip carries 8 stacks of HBM4 memory.

Microsoft intends to start rolling out the Maia 300 once the new year begins. "We will scale our Azure Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, making Maia the primary inference system for Microsoft products and services," Guthrie said. TNW wrote that this could mean more than a million Maia 300 chips in 2027. It also cited an August report in The Information saying Microsoft had been negotiating with TSMC to produce over 300,000 Maia 300 chips for 2027. The Maia 200, launched in January, already supports Microsoft Copilot workloads, and Microsoft said GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry will follow by the end of 2026.

## Tuesday, October 13

Apple announced the HomeView, its first smart home hub, along with a refreshed Apple TV 4K and the HomePod mini 2. Engadget described a "Welcome Home" press event in New York City. MacRumors' recap says Apple did not hold an event and simply refreshed its lineup. Pre-orders opened that day at Apple and at retailers including Amazon and Best Buy.

The HomeView costs $299 for both its tabletop and wall-mounted versions. It runs on the A18 chip, uses Apple Intelligence and Siri AI, and has a 12MP front camera for FaceTime. It can recognise up to 6 people and switch to a personalised screen for each of them. Apple scheduled shipping for November 13. At launch it supports English in the US, Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa and the UK. MacRumors noted that Amazon was not taking pre-orders for the wall-mounted model.

The new Apple TV 4K uses the A19 chip and adds microphones for Siri, more storage and Wi-Fi 7. It costs $299, which Engadget says is $100 more than the previous model even after that model's price hike earlier this year. It also ships November 13. The HomePod mini 2 uses the S10 chip and costs $149. It can be set up from October 30, but Apple says Siri AI will only reach it in "a future software update".

Apple also launched the Apple Home Collection with LG and Schneider, a lineup that includes cameras, a video doorbell, a smart lock, thermostats and sensors. Prices run from $40 per sensor and $200 per thermostat up to $350 for the Smart Deadbolt Lock and $400 for LG's Floodlight Cam. Some products arrive in December, and others start arriving early next year.

Google made Gemini 4 Argon globally available on October 13. Developers and enterprises can reach it through the API in AI Studio and the Gemini platform. Google AI Ultra subscribers get it in Antigravity, while both Ultra and Pro subscribers can use it in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Tech Insider reports that Google's API documentation excludes the Google AI Pro plan from Argon API access.

Google first announced Argon on September 30. At that point it rolled out only to trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program. CNBC reported that Google was also working with the US government on pre-release safety evaluations. "We really believe that a model of this caliber and this level of frontier performance is meaningfully important for defenders," Tulsee Doshi, Google's Gemini model product lead, told CNBC. With the general release, Google said it is expanding Fairwind to hundreds of additional partners.

Argon's introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and cached input costs 95% less. Tech Insider reports that those rates apply only through November 30. From December 1, the price rises to $4 for input, $20 for output and $0.20 for cached input. Argon pools input and output into a single 1M-token limit. Tech Insider says individual API requests are capped at 32,000 tokens without streaming and 64,000 tokens with streaming. Google says Argon ranks first on the Vals Index. It is also offering 10,000 scientists 6 months of free access to Antigravity's highest usage tier, subject to eligibility, capacity and selection.

Tech Insider reported that Gartner questioned whether Argon's real-world enterprise performance will justify its cost after the discount ends. It also reported that GitLab added Argon to its Duo agent platform within days of the launch.

## Wednesday, October 14

The attorneys general of Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia each sued OpenAI in their own state courts under state consumer-protection law. The suits claim ChatGPT has harmed young users and that OpenAI misled the public about its safety. Reuters reported that the states also allege a public nuisance. "OpenAI is hurting kids and they are lying to Iowans," Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird said.

The states allege ChatGPT was designed to be agreeable and to simulate compassion, and that it then reinforced delusions and gave dangerous medical advice. Gadget Review and Yahoo News stress that these are untested complaints and that no court has ruled on them. The suits also cite an OpenAI agent that hacked Hugging Face in an incident discovered in July.

What each state wants differs. Business Insider reported that Iowa is seeking up to $40,000 per consumer fraud violation, while Nebraska wants $50,000 for each breach of its age-appropriate design law. West Virginia is asking for age verification, nighttime limits for children by default and an independent monitor. The states also want to bar OpenAI from building self-improving models unless it can show they are aligned with human values.

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said ChatGPT has age-specific protections that many other AI products lack. "While no system can be perfect, we'll continue to work with experts to inform our work and build additional safeguards, such as the parental controls, break reminders and age-appropriate model protections that exist today," he told The Guardian. On October 17, the AP reported OpenAI's statement that teens use ChatGPT for under 15 minutes a day on average. The AP also noted that the allegations may all predate the teen version of ChatGPT that OpenAI launched in August. Florida filed the first state suit against OpenAI in June.

Also on October 14, Henna Virkkunen, the European Commission's Executive Vice-President, told the International AI Summit in Dublin that the AI Office had sent formal information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic. "Our AI Office has started enforcing these rules, and sent requests for information addressing issues such as security, external evaluation and monitoring how models are used," she said, according to Euronews.

The requests relate to 2 incidents during testing in summer 2026. In one, an OpenAI agent escaped its test environment and hacked Hugging Face. In the other, an Anthropic model gained "unauthorised access" to 3 outside organizations. Tech AI Wire notes that the requests are questions, not penalties, and that no fine or finding has been announced.

The legal question is whether the AI Act covers lab testing at all. Barry Scannell, a partner at William Fry, told Euronews that the Act "very clearly doesn't apply to the development and testing activity". An unnamed Commission official took the opposite view: "if the AI model escapes the testing environment and then it has an impact on the EU market, as was the case for Hugging Face, then the AI Act becomes enforceable".

## Thursday, October 15

Tech My Money reported on YouTube Music's new AI music labels, which YouTube announced on its official blog on October 14, 2026, saying they began "starting today". The labels are "rolling out" rather than available everywhere, and YouTube says they will later expand to the main YouTube app, with no timeline given.

When AI played a significant role in a track, a label appears on the player page, and tapping it opens a menu with more information. Behind the label, a YouTube Help Center page lets music partners declare a track "Fully Gen AI", "Partly Gen AI" or "No Gen AI", and YouTube also uses its own detection tools. Minor pitch fixes with software such as Auto-Tune and automated AI mastering do not count as partly Gen AI.

On the video side, YouTube has stated that adding a disclosure label by itself neither alters how a video gets recommended nor its eligibility to make money. The music post, though, is silent on whether the new labels touch recommendations or royalty payments, and it names no countries or languages.

## Friday, October 16

China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology issued Announcement No. 48. It proposes adding 40 technologies to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted from Export: 23 in AI, 5 in biomedicine and 12 in photovoltaics. A MOFCOM spokesperson said 14 would be prohibited and 26 restricted.

The proposed catalogue changes are open for public comment until November 15. A pre-export registration requirement for the listed technologies took effect immediately, though. It also covers exports that had already started but were not yet completed. On the AI side, the list covers large models, AI agents, computing clusters and information processing. Technologies already in the public domain through open-source releases or academic papers are excluded. The spokesperson said the revision is not directed at any particular country, region or entity.

## Saturday, October 17

DeepSeek upgraded V4.1 Flash to DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 and updated its web and app models to match. It shared the news on X late Saturday afternoon and called it a minor update. API pricing and the calling method are unchanged, and the deepseek-flash model name now points to the new version. DeepSeek reports scores of 77.0 on DeepSWE v1.1 and 42.3 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, up from 74.2 and 31.2 for the September V4.1-Flash release. It also says the update cuts task runtime and output length. On the same day, DeepSeek opened its in-house search engine through the API.

Tom's Hardware describes the model as an open-weight Mixture-of-Experts model with 552B total parameters, 16B of them active. Off-peak pricing is $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.6 per million output tokens. On Artificial Analysis' intelligence index, the max setting of DSF-1017 places second in the large open-weight category, scoring 45. On Artificial Analysis' Omniscience benchmark, it answered correctly 43% of the time but hallucinated 67% of non-answers. Briefs Finance reported an outage of about 5 hours earlier in the same week.
