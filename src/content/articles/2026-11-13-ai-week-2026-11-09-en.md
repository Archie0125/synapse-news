*Written on November 13, 2026, covering news from Nov 9–12, 2026.*

Two frontier model launches, an IPO roadshow and a founder-CEO exit made for a crowded week. Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on Monday, Google followed with Gemini 4 Carbon on Tuesday, and on Thursday Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned. The threads connect: Nvidia is a $10 billion investor in Anthropic, and Anthropic says it runs Claude on NVIDIA GPUs alongside other chips.

## Monday, November 9

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. Fable 5.2 became generally available that day, while Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted organizations through Anthropic's Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. MacRumors adds that Mythos is also offered via Claude Security on Enterprise plans.

According to MacRumors, both run on the same underlying model, but the public-facing Fable 5.2 carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards intended to prevent misuse. It reports that Fable 5.2 produces approximately 55 percent fewer blocks than Fable 5.1.

Anthropic says the new versions generate outputs up to 30 percent faster than the 5.1 models. CNBC reports they cost 20% less than the 5.1 generation, priced at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Pro plans and standard Team seats can spend up to 50 percent of their weekly usage limits on Fable.

Anthropic's advice is to begin with Opus 5.5 for the bulk of coding and knowledge work, and to save Fable and Mythos for "the most ambitious, open-ended technical work". Dianne Penn, Anthropic's head of product management for research, told CNBC that Fable 5.2 is mainly aimed at "power users or power use cases" in hard engineering, mathematics and robotics problems.

CNBC notes this is Anthropic's first frontier release since CEO Dario Amodei called in September for slowing the pace of AI development. "We called for pacing, not pausing," Penn said. Anthropic said the models went through safety and alignment testing and that it built new evaluations and post-launch monitoring tools before release.

CNBC also reported that day that the IPO could happen as soon as next week, with Anthropic targeting a valuation of $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion, below a $2 trillion figure floated in recent months. Its amended prospectus shows $33.24 billion of revenue across the nine months to September 30, roughly 1,200% higher than the same stretch a year before.

## Tuesday, November 10

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises, scientists and consumers. Paying developers and enterprises get API access via Google AI Studio and Gemini for business, priced like Gemini 4 Argon's introductory tier: input costs $2 and output $10, each per million tokens. Google AI Ultra subscribers are also getting it in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search and Google Antigravity. Engadget puts the Ultra membership at $100 a month.

Carbon had a narrower debut first: Google says it gave the model to a set of cybersecurity defenders through its Fairwind Program two weeks earlier, which Engadget places in late October. Google says Argon remains the better pick for most software engineering and knowledge work, while Carbon targets harder jobs such as multi-step coding, debugging and open-ended scientific research, spending more tokens than Argon to get there.

Google says Carbon tops CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, and that its cost per task is less than half that of the strongest external rival. It also previewed /swarm in Google Antigravity, which spins one prompt into a coordinated group of agents that users can track live.

On security work, Google says Wiz used Carbon to scan two major SaaS providers and the model found 0-day vulnerabilities in under two hours. On safety, Google reports Carbon blocked 99% of attack trajectories from an adaptive red-teaming agent in simulated testing, with a per-attempt breach rate of 0.8%, down from 3.1% on Argon.

Google is also extending its free-access program for 10,000 scientists, who get six months of no-cost access in Antigravity, to cover Carbon; it says thousands have already been onboarded.

## Wednesday, November 11

On November 11, Simon Willison published highlights and a transcript he extracted from Anthropic's IPO roadshow video, saying it included previously unreleased details about how the business works. Anthropic had formally launched the roadshow on November 10.

The proposed offering covers 825,000,000 Class A shares: 801,351,354 sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by certain existing stockholders, whose sale brings Anthropic no proceeds. Underwriters are expected to get a 30-day option on up to 123,750,000 more shares. The expected price range is $70.00 to $78.00 per share, and Anthropic has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under "ANT". The registration statement filed with the SEC has not yet become effective.

The video's compute section features co-founder Tom Brown and describes training and serving Claude across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and AMD GPUs. "We train and serve Claude now across all four major chip families," the video says. It says Anthropic began working with AMD GPUs this year, and that Mythos got Claude running on AMD in three days "with basically no human intervention," with Claude porting or writing almost 100% of the kernels.

The video also pitches "compute multipliers": in its example, a 4x multiplier means a model needing 100 units of compute could instead be trained with 25. It claims that Claude "will be able to discover compute multipliers entirely by itself".

## Thursday, November 12

Nvidia's board announced that Huang resigned as President, CEO and board member, effective immediately. TechCrunch, citing a regulatory filing, reports the board accepted the resignation on Wednesday. TechCrunch says the resignation came days after Huang admitted to a romantic affair with an alleged Chinese spy, which it says set off a U.S. national security crisis.

CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and will keep her CFO role until a permanent successor is named; the board will consider internal and external candidates. Kress has been CFO since joining in September 2013, after nearly 25 years in finance roles at companies including Cisco and Microsoft. Former Synopsys CEO Harvey Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair, and TechCrunch says he has sat on the board since 1993. Kress said she is "committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years".

TechCrunch reports Nvidia shares fell nearly 12% in early Thursday trading and as much as 19% since Friday, a market cap loss of more than $855 billion. CoreWeave fell about 9%, Nebius about 16% and IREN 6.6%. TechCrunch also lists Nvidia's exposure: $99 billion in investments plus $25 billion in commitments as of its second quarter, $36 billion in cloud service commitments, $30 billion put into OpenAI, $10 billion into Anthropic and $3.5 billion in MediaTek.

TechCrunch notes Huang owns only about 3.5% of shares without supervoting rights, ran a flat organization with around 60 direct reports, and left no heir apparent or succession plan.
