AI wearables are making privacy contagious: one buyer’s convenience can quietly conscript everyone nearby into a recording system they never chose.

## Key points

- Smart glasses from Meta and Google can capture what wearers see or use AI to describe their surroundings, extending data collection into ordinary physical spaces.
- Apple’s Audio Intelligence and Amazon’s Bee devices can record, transcribe, or summarize daily conversations and activities.
- Always-on pendants, rings, and other AI wearables make bystanders part of a customer’s privacy decision without requiring their meaningful consent.
- The burden is shifting toward ordinary people, who may need to recognize numerous devices and understand their recording capabilities simply to protect themselves.
- Privacy controls designed around individual choice are inadequate when one person’s device can continuously collect information about everyone nearby.

## Why it matters

AI wearables promise a personal assistant, but their hidden business model is socializing the privacy cost: the owner gets convenience while every bystander supplies the data.
