### **Nvidia privately confirmed it could track chips to fight smuggling, even as industry publicly questioned feasibility**

Nvidia representatives told Illinois representative Bill Foster that it was possible to implement the anti-smuggling measures called for in the Chip Security Act, Foster told *MIT Technology Review* on Thursday, despite an industry group claiming they were “potentially infeasible.”

Foster, a Democrat who serves on the Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, is a cosponsor of the act, which would introduce geotracking requirements to verify the location of advanced AI chips and prevent their smuggling to China.

The act is drawing renewed attention following Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation and reported affair. ### **More autonomy, fewer NDAs: Volunteers have new requests for brain implant trials**

Scientists have been working on ways to decode brain activity for decades. Teams have developed brain implants that can control robotic limbs and computers, and even “speak.”

The people who volunteer for trials of these devices play a vital role in that research, and they ought to be recognized for their contributions, says Ian Burkhart, who had one implanted in his own brain back in 2014.

He’s been working on a set of guidelines for ethical trials to respect participants’ autonomy—and access their own brain data whenever they want.

## Summary

**TLDR:** Silicon Valley’s political misread and Nvidia’s chip-tracking contradiction share one lesson: public tech narratives deserve less trust than private incentives.

- The newsletter highlights technology leaders’ support for Trump’s AI agenda as a costly failure to read the midterm political environment.
- It also reports that Nvidia representatives privately told Representative Bill Foster that anti-smuggling chip tracking was technically possible.
- That private assessment conflicts with an industry group’s public claim that the proposed measures could be infeasible.
- The Chip Security Act would require geolocation controls to verify where advanced AI chips are operating and curb smuggling to China.
- Both stories expose a credibility problem: an industry selling prediction and technical certainty can become evasive when policy threatens its interests.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** 矽谷的政治誤判與 Nvidia 的晶片追蹤矛盾指向同一件事：科技業的公開說法，往往不如私下誘因可信。

- 這期電子報先指出，科技領袖支持川普人工智慧議程，是對期中選舉政治風向的一次昂貴誤判。
- 另一則報導稱，Nvidia 代表曾私下告訴眾議員 Bill Foster，防走私所需的晶片追蹤技術確實可行。
- 這與產業團體公開聲稱相關措施可能無法實施的說法形成衝突。
- 《晶片安全法案》擬要求先進人工智慧晶片具備地理位置驗證能力，以阻止晶片走私至中國。
- 兩則故事共同暴露可信度問題：當政策觸及自身利益，最愛談預測與技術能力的產業也會改口。
