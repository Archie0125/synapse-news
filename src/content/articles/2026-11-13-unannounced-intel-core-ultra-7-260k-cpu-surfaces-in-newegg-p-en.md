**Update 11/13/26 at 12:10pm ET:** Intel has confirmed the 260K Plus as a "limited-availability" SKU and says it's not currently available as a boxed retail chip. Specs should go live on Intel's website within the next few days.

A new Intel Arrow Lake SKU is listed for sale in multiple prebuilt PCs on Newegg, despite no official acknowledgement of the model from Intel. The Core Ultra 7 260K is a downclocked version of the Core Ultra 7 265K from Intel's main Arrow Lake stack, not the Arrow Lake Refresh stack that's infiltrated our list of the [best CPUs for gaming](https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/best-cpus,3986.html). Outside of a few prebuilt listings on Newegg's website, you'll find no mention of the CPU elsewhere online (including Intel's official [Core Ultra Series 2 specs page](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/ark/products/series/241071/intel-core-ultra-processors-series-2.html)).

Although retailer-exclusive and late-generation variants are common from AMD and Intel — we recently reviewed the [Ryzen 5 5500F](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-ryzen-5-5500f-review), for instance — they almost never fly completely under the radar, as is the case with the Core Ultra 7 260K. The chip itself is identical to the Core Ultra 7 265K based on the specs we know, fitted with 20 cores, eight of which are P-cores. The 260K tops out at 5.4 GHz, however, while the 265K reaches 5.5 GHz.

Otherwise, the specs are all the same. It comes with the same 125W TDP, same 30 MB of L3 cache, and same support for up to 256 GB of memory running at DDR5-6400. We only know of these specs from an image shown on the two ABS prebuilt listings available with the 260K. It's available in the [ABS Kaze II paired with an RTX 5060](https://click.linksynergy.com/deeplink?id=kXQk6%2AivFEQ&mid=44583&u1=tomshardware-us-1143568960456148809&murl=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.newegg.com%2Fabs-kaze-ii-white-gaming-desktop-pcs-geforce-rtx-5060-intel-core-ultra-7-260k-32gb-ddr5-1tb-nvme-ssd-k2a57n56r1%2Fp%2FN82E16883362061), as well as the [Flux II Aqua with an RTX 5070](https://click.linksynergy.com/deeplink?id=kXQk6%2AivFEQ&mid=44583&u1=tomshardware-us-2179964502648801500&murl=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.newegg.com%2Fabs-flux-ii-gaming-desktop-pc-geforce-rtx-5070-intel-core-ultra-7-260k-32gb-ddr5-1tb-nvme-ssd-f2a57n57r1-black%2Fp%2FN82E16883362062).

The image is below, captured hastily, as you can see, from a [Microsoft](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/microsoft) Teams call. Again, we weren't able to find any information from Intel about the chip outside of this slide.

One critical detail this slide confirms is that the Core Ultra 7 260K belongs to the Arrow Lake family, not the range of Arrow Lake Refresh processors like the [Core Ultra 7 270K Plus](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/intel-core-ultra-7-270k-plus-review/2). It tops out at DDR5-6400, while Arrow Lake Refresh climbs up to DDR5-7200. That means the chip doesn't have access to the hardware-level enhancements available in Arrow Lake Refresh, nor the [software-based Intel Binary Optimization Tool](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/intels-binary-optimization-tool-tested-and-explained-how-the-ibot-translation-delivers-up-to-18-percent-faster-gaming-performance-8-percent-on-average) that boosts performance in select games.

Although that's enough to categorize the 260K, there are some details that are unclear. In the two PCs it's available in, the chip is referred to as the 260K across the full product page *except* in Newegg's option selector (pictured below), where it's referred to as the U7-260KF. Intel's KF models are unlocked for overclocking but lack integrated graphics.

Additionally, the slide from Intel referenced above shows the chip boosting up to 5.4 GHz, but the product page lists the chip at 5.2 GHz for maximum boost clock.

## Summary

**TLDR:** Intel’s nearly invisible 260K Plus looks less like innovation than channel segmentation: a 100MHz trim packaged for selected prebuilt buyers.

- The processor appeared in ABS gaming desktops on Newegg before Intel publicly documented it.
- Intel later confirmed the Core Ultra 7 260K Plus as a limited-availability SKU that is not currently sold as a boxed retail chip.
- The listed specifications include 20 cores with eight P-cores, a 125W TDP, 30MB of L3 cache, and DDR5-6400 support.
- Its reported maximum clock is 5.4GHz, only 100MHz below the Core Ultra 7 265K’s 5.5GHz.
- The unusual launch suggests Intel is using a minimally differentiated chip to serve selected system-builder or retailer channels.

## 摘要

**重點摘要：** Intel 幾乎無預告推出 260K Plus，與其說是新品創新，不如說是把少 100MHz 的晶片切給特定整機通路。

- 這款處理器先出現在 Newegg 的 ABS 電競桌機中，當時 Intel 官網仍找不到正式資料。
- Intel 後來確認 Core Ultra 7 260K Plus 是限量供應型號，目前不會以盒裝零售處理器販售。
- 已知規格包括 20 核心、其中八個為效能核心，另有 125W TDP、30MB L3 快取與 DDR5-6400 支援。
- 最高時脈據稱為 5.4GHz，只比 Core Ultra 7 265K 的 5.5GHz 少 100MHz。
- 這種低調上架方式顯示產品主要服務特定系統整合商或零售通路，而非建立新的效能級距。
