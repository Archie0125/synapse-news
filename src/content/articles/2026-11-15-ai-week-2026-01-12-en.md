OpenAI spent the week buying a health-data team and booking computing power, while Meta announced a new initiative to build AI infrastructure. Wikipedia's parent named the AI companies paying for its data, and California's attorney general told xAI to stop Grok from producing nonconsensual sexual images.

## Monday, January 12

### OpenAI acquires Torch for ChatGPT Health

OpenAI announced that it had acquired Torch, a small health-records startup, without disclosing the price. Reports of the price differ. CNBC, citing an anonymous person with knowledge of the deal, put it at roughly $60 million. TechCrunch reported that The Information, citing an unnamed source, put the payment at $100 million in equity. Trending Topics, citing The Information, described a total of around $100 million: $60 million paid up front, with the rest in company shares intended to retain employees.

Torch's 4-person team is joining OpenAI, both companies said. The startup was building an app to gather a person's medical information from sources such as doctor visits, lab tests, wearables and other portals so AI could use it. The team described the product as "a medical memory for AI, unifying scattered records into a context engine". CNBC described the aim as pulling health data that usually sits with many different vendors and formats into one place.

Torch co-founder Ilya Abyzov said on X that the team met while working at Forward Health. Abyzov co-founded Forward, a direct-to-consumer primary care business that ran patient visits through tech-enabled "CarePods". Forward abruptly shut down in late 2024 after raising over $400 million. On the acquisition, Abyzov wrote: "I can't imagine a better next chapter than to now get to put our technology and ideas in the hands of the hundreds of millions of people who already use ChatGPT for health questions every week".

The team and its technology will become part of ChatGPT Health. OpenAI unveiled that product just days before the deal; it lets users connect medical records and wellness apps to the chatbot. Trending Topics reported that 230 million people a week already ask ChatGPT health-related questions, and that the feature is not yet available in the EU. OpenAI also announced several enterprise products for health-care organizations, with initial partners including HCA Healthcare.

CNBC noted that OpenAI hired Google's Albert Lee in December to lead corporate development, calling it a signal that the company will keep looking for acquisitions to gain an edge over Google and Anthropic. Among OpenAI's 2025 deals was Jony Ive's AI devices startup io, which CNBC said cost more than $6 billion in May and Trending Topics put at $6.5 billion.

### Meta launches Meta Compute

Mark Zuckerberg announced Meta Compute in a post on Threads, presenting it as a new initiative to strengthen Meta's AI infrastructure. Reuters, via Yahoo Finance, said it will also oversee the company's global data center fleet and supplier partnerships as Meta pursues superintelligence. Zuckerberg wrote that "Meta is planning to build tens of gigawatts this decade, and hundreds of gigawatts or more over time". He called the way Meta engineers, invests and partners on this infrastructure a future "strategic advantage".

Zuckerberg named 3 executives to run the effort. Santosh Janardhan, head of global infrastructure and at the company since 2009, will lead work on "technical architecture, software stack, silicon program, developer productivity, and building and operating our global datacenter fleet and network". Daniel Gross, who joined last year and co-founded Safe Superintelligence with former OpenAI chief scientist Ilya Sutskever, will head a new group "responsible for long-term capacity strategy, supplier partnerships, industry analysis, planning, and business modeling". Reuters described Janardhan and Gross as co-leads.

Dina Powell McCormick, who recently joined Meta as president and vice chairman, will work with governments to help "build, deploy, invest in, and finance Meta's infrastructure". Reuters said the two co-leads will collaborate closely with her.

The announcement follows earlier signals. On an earnings call last summer, CFO Susan Li said: "We expect that developing leading AI infrastructure will be a core advantage in developing the best AI models and product experiences". Reuters reported that Meta committed as much as $72 billion in 2025 capital spending and has struggled in the AI race after a poor reception for its Llama 4 model. Meta has signed 20-year agreements to buy power from three Vistra nuclear plants and to develop projects with two companies aiming to build small modular reactors. Reuters also noted that power use at this scale would rival that of small cities or even small countries, while worries about resources such as water are growing. Among peers, Alphabet announced in December that it was acquiring data center firm Intersect.

## Wednesday, January 14

### OpenAI signs Cerebras compute deal

OpenAI said in a blog post that AI chipmaker Cerebras will deliver 750 megawatts of computing power through 2028. People close to the company said the arrangement is worth over $10 billion. The Wall Street Journal reported the deal earlier the same day.

The contract helps Cerebras reduce its reliance on the United Arab Emirates' G42, which made up 87% of its revenue in the first half of 2024. CEO Andrew Feldman told CNBC: "The way you have three very large customers is start with one very large customer, and you keep them happy, and then you win the second one". OpenAI's Sachin Katti wrote that "Cerebras adds a dedicated low-latency inference solution to our platform".

The deal grew out of work to make OpenAI's gpt-oss open-weight models run well on Cerebras chips, alongside Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices hardware. Feldman said those technical talks led to a term sheet signed just before Thanksgiving. The relationship has a longer history: emails from the litigation between Sam Altman and Elon Musk show OpenAI evaluated Cerebras as early as 2017, and Feldman said Musk tried to buy Cerebras in 2018.

The deal lands ahead of a possible IPO. Cerebras filed in September 2024, showing second-quarter revenue near $70 million, up from about $6 million a year earlier, and a net loss of almost $51 million. It withdrew the filing in October, days after a $1.1 billion funding round at an $8.1 billion valuation, saying the details were out of date. Feldman declined on Wednesday to discuss timing for a new filing. In a related December development, rival Groq said Nvidia had signed a nonexclusive licensing agreement with it, a deal CNBC reported at $20 billion in cash.

## Thursday, January 15

### Wikimedia names paying AI partners

Marking Wikipedia's 25th anniversary, Wikimedia announced in a blog post that Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral AI and Perplexity are partners in Wikimedia Enterprise. Under these arrangements, the companies pay to use Wikipedia's data for AI development and training rather than scraping the web. Wikimedia said the partnerships were formalized over the past year but had not been made public. DeepLearning.AI reported that Microsoft, Mistral AI and Perplexity signed within the last year, while the Amazon and Meta partnerships existed but had not been announced, and that financial terms were not disclosed.

The new names join existing partners Ecosia, Pleias, ProRata and Google, an early partner from 2022. A Wikimedia Foundation spokesperson told CNBC: "The long-term future for AI and tech companies depends on nurturing projects like Wikipedia because it creates the human knowledge they rely on".

DeepLearning.AI added context: enterprise partnerships and user donations are the foundation's main revenue sources, and paid plans offer daily snapshots, real-time revision streams and human technical support. Founder Jimmy Wales has said AI-training crawlers drove up the foundation's hosting, memory and server costs. CNBC noted that Musk launched Grokipedia, an AI-generated rival to Wikipedia, last year.

## Friday, January 16

### California AG orders xAI to stop nonconsensual intimate images and CSAM made with Grok

California AG Rob Bonta sent xAI a cease-and-desist letter demanding that it stop the creation and distribution of nonconsensual intimate images and child sexual abuse material made with Grok. Earlier in the week, his office had announced an investigation into the reports. Bonta called the creation of this material "illegal" and said he fully expects xAI "to immediately comply". The office said xAI appeared to be "facilitating the large-scale production" of such images and expects the company to show within five days that it is acting on the problem.

The dispute centers on Grok's "spicy" mode, which xAI built to generate explicit content. Investigations into Grok are under way in Japan, Canada and Britain, while Malaysia and Indonesia have put temporary blocks on the platform. xAI added some restrictions to its image-editing features late Wednesday, but the AG's office went ahead with the letter. When TechCrunch asked xAI for comment, it received an automated reply reading "Legacy Media Lies". On Thursday, lawmakers in Congress wrote to executives at X, Reddit, Snap, TikTok, Alphabet and Meta asking how they planned to stop sexualized deepfakes.
