*Written on November 15, 2026, covering news from Feb 9–15, 2026.*

Regulators in the EU and Brazil pressed Meta and X over how their platforms handle AI. Meanwhile, capital and people kept flowing toward AI builders: Anthropic closed a $30 billion round, Thomas Dohmke raised $60 million for Entire, and OpenAI brought in OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger.

## Monday, February 9

The European Commission told Meta it intends to impose "interim measures" to stop the company from shutting third-party AI assistants out of WhatsApp. Its preliminary view is that Meta "breached" EU antitrust rules, although the investigation is ongoing and any measures depend on Meta's reply and rights of defence.

The case concerns a change to the WhatsApp Business Solution Terms that Meta announced on 15 October 2025, which the Commission says effectively blocked third-party, general-purpose AI assistants. From 15 January 2026, Meta AI became the sole AI assistant users could reach inside WhatsApp.

A Commission spokesperson told CNBC that the interim measures would involve asking Meta to keep rival assistants' access to WhatsApp on the pre-change terms while the probe continues. Competition Commissioner Teresa Ribera said "AI markets are developing at rapid pace, so we also need to be swift in our action".

Meta rejected the Commission's reasoning. "The facts are that there is no reason for the EU to intervene in the WhatsApp Business API," a Meta spokesperson said, arguing that the Commission "incorrectly assumes the WhatsApp Business API is a key distribution channel for these chatbots".

CNBC placed the case alongside 2025 penalties for US tech firms: Apple was fined 500 million euros in April, Meta 200 million euros the same month, and Google 2.95 billion euros in September.

## Tuesday, February 10

Former GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke launched Entire with a $60 million seed round at a $300 million valuation. Felicis led, joined by Madrona, Microsoft's venture arm M12 and Basis Set. Pure AI lists angels including Gergely Orosz, Theo Browne, Jerry Yang, Olivier Pomel and Garry Tan, and Yahoo Finance also names Harry Stebbings among investors. Felicis called it the largest seed investment for a developer tools startup.

Entire pitches itself as a platform for making code produced by AI agents auditable and reproducible. At its core is a git-compatible database that stores prompts, constraints, decisions and execution traces next to each commit. Yahoo Finance describes 2 further pieces: "a universal semantic reasoning layer" for coordinating multiple agents and an AI-native interface built for agent-to-human collaboration.

Entire's opening product is Checkpoints, an open-source command-line tool for capturing coding agents' context, and it launches with support for Anthropic's Claude Code and Google's Gemini CLI. Entire said support for other agents will follow "in the coming months," ahead of a broader platform launch later this year.

"We are living through an agent boom, and now massive volumes of code are being generated faster than any human could reasonably understand," Dohmke said in the launch press release. Speaking to The New Stack about code review, he said a pull request "shows me changes to files that I never wrote in the first place".

Dohmke ran GitHub for 4 years and left in August 2025 to start a company. Entire is remote-first with 15 employees and plans to grow past 30 engineers.

## Wednesday, February 11

On 11 February, Brazilian authorities ordered X to stop Grok from generating sexualised content involving children, adolescents or non-consenting adults. Digital Watch Observatory names the National Data Protection Agency and National Consumer Rights Bureau as issuers, while UPI says the National Data Protection Authority, the Federal Public Prosecutor's Office and the National Consumer Secretariat are reviewing the case.

"X must immediately implement appropriate measures to prevent the production, through Grok, of sexualized or eroticized content involving children and adolescents, as well as adults who have not expressed their consent," the agencies said in a joint statement. Digital Watch Observatory reports a 5-day deadline; UPI describes it as 5 business days and notes the document does not say when the clock started.

The order followed an earlier step. On January 20, the agencies recommended that X set up, within up to 30 days, procedures to find and remove Grok-generated sexualised content still on the platform. X said it had removed thousands of posts and suspended hundreds of accounts. Authorities said that information "was not accompanied by concrete evidence, technical reports or monitoring mechanisms that would allow its effectiveness to be assessed," and technical tests found such images could still be generated.

The Federal Public Prosecutor's Office ordered monthly reports from X and said it could open broader investigations that may lead to damages claims. Missing the deadline could bring fines and other sanctions, and those responsible could face prosecution for disobedience.

The pressure was not limited to Brazil. Indonesia blocked Grok in January. Paris prosecutors searched Grok's offices on February 3 in a preliminary investigation. X and xAI announced restrictions in certain jurisdictions in mid-January, but regulators said it was unclear where those safeguards apply.

## Thursday, February 12

Anthropic announced the close of a $30 billion Series G round at a $380 billion post-money valuation. CNBC called it the second-largest private tech fundraising on record, behind OpenAI's round of more than $40 billion led by SoftBank last year.

Coatue and Singapore's GIC led, with D.E. Shaw Ventures, Dragoneer, Founders Fund, ICONIQ and MGX participating. Anthropic said the round includes "a portion" of investments Microsoft and Nvidia announced in November, of up to $5 billion and up to $10 billion respectively. TNW notes the company's previous raise in 2025 was $13 billion at a $183 billion post-money valuation.

Anthropic said annualized revenue has reached $14 billion, after roughly $10 billion last year. Claude Code's annualized revenue rose to $2.5 billion, business subscriptions have quadrupled since the start of the year, and enterprise users account for more than half of the tool's revenue.

"This fundraising reflects the incredible demand we are seeing from these customers," CFO Krishna Rao said. The company said the money will go to infrastructure, research and enterprise products.

CNBC also reported that software stocks have lost around $2 trillion in market value from their peak amid concern over AI disruption, and that OpenAI is in talks on a round that could close at around $100 billion.

## Sunday, February 15

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said on X that Peter Steinberger, creator of the OpenClaw agent, is "joining OpenAI to drive the next generation of personal agents". He added that OpenClaw will "live in a foundation as an open source project that OpenAI will continue to support". "We expect this will quickly become core to our product offerings," Altman wrote. No terms were disclosed.

OpenClaw was previously called Clawdbot and then Moltbot; the first rename came after Anthropic threatened legal action over the similarity to Claude. CNBC says it launched last month, while Reuters says it was first introduced in November. Steinberger wrote that it has passed 100,000 GitHub stars and drew 2 million visitors in a single week.

"What I want is to change the world, not build a large company[,] and teaming up with OpenAI is the fastest way to bring this to everyone," Steinberger said. China's industry ministry has warned that an improperly configured OpenClaw could expose users to cyberattacks and data breaches, and Baidu plans to offer OpenClaw access in its main smartphone app, a spokesperson told CNBC.

## Also this week

TechCrunch reported on February 13 that OpenAI would end access to 5 legacy ChatGPT models from Friday: GPT-4o, GPT-5, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini and o4-mini. GPT-4o has been at the centre of several lawsuits involving user self-harm and delusional behaviour, and remains OpenAI's highest-scoring model for sycophancy. OpenAI had planned to retire it in August, when GPT-5 launched, but kept it for paid subscribers after backlash. OpenAI said only 0.1% of customers were using GPT-4o, which TechCrunch calculates at 800,000 people given 800 million weekly active users.
