*Written on November 15, 2026, covering news from Feb 23–Mar 1, 2026.*

Anthropic opened the week accusing 3 Chinese AI labs of mining Claude through roughly 24,000 fraudulent accounts, and closed it with President Trump ordering every federal agency to stop using its technology. The fight came down to 2 limits Anthropic would not give up: no mass domestic surveillance and no fully autonomous weapons. Meanwhile, Meta signed up for up to 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs.

## Tuesday, February 24

AMD and Meta announced a multi-year, multi-generation agreement for Meta to deploy up to 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs. The first deployment pairs a custom Instinct GPU based on the MI450 architecture with 6th Gen EPYC CPUs codenamed "Venice", ROCm software and the Helios rack-scale design, with shipments for the first gigawatt slated to start in the second half of 2026. CNBC said early MI450 shipments in Helios servers will begin later this year.

The deal comes with a performance-based warrant giving Meta up to 160 million AMD shares. TechCrunch put that at about 10% of the company, at $0.01 per share. The first tranche vests with the initial 1 gigawatt of shipments and further tranches as purchases rise toward 6 gigawatts; vesting is also tied to AMD stock price thresholds, and exercise is tied to Meta hitting technical and commercial milestones. TechCrunch, citing The Wall Street Journal, reported that the final tranche requires AMD shares to reach $600. AMD closed at $196.60 on Monday, according to TechCrunch.

On value, the reports diverge. TechCrunch said Meta could buy potentially up to $100 billion of AMD chips. CNBC said terms were not provided, and chip analyst Ben Bajarin of Creative Strategies, who was briefed, estimated the deal at tens of billions of dollars over at least four years.

Mark Zuckerberg called it "an important step for Meta as we diversify our compute". Lisa Su told CNBC, "This is about making the right bets at the right time". At an investor briefing she added, "The CPU market is absolutely on fire". AMD CFO Jean Hu said the company expects the partnership to be accretive to non-GAAP earnings per share. Meta will also be a lead customer for the "Venice" and "Verano" EPYC processors.

AMD stock rose 7%, Meta traded slightly lower and Nvidia was largely flat. CNBC noted the deal landed a week after Meta committed to millions of Nvidia processors, and that AMD's October deal with OpenAI also involved warrants for 160 million shares. Bajarin pointed to the customized GPUs as a difference from Meta's Nvidia pact: "We don't have any indication Nvidia is doing that". CNBC put Nvidia at roughly 90% of the AI chip market with a $4.66 trillion valuation, against AMD's $320 billion.

## Wednesday, February 25

Business Times, citing people familiar with the discussion who spoke to Axios, reported that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth delivered an ultimatum to Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at a February 25 meeting at the Pentagon. ABC News, citing a Pentagon official and a source familiar with the matter, described the meeting as taking place on Tuesday. Both outlets reported a Friday deadline. Business Times put the cutoff at 5:01 on Friday evening and said the threats included cancelling a $200 million contract and pursuing a "supply chain risk" designation. ABC News reported that Hegseth said he would invoke the Defense Production Act if Anthropic did not comply.

According to ABC's source, Amodei laid out Anthropic's red lines: autonomous weapons in which AI rather than humans makes final targeting decisions, and mass domestic surveillance of Americans. The Pentagon official said the dispute had nothing to do with those uses: "Legality is the Pentagon's responsibility as the end user". Business Times reported the department wants Claude for "all lawful purposes" without Anthropic holding a veto, and that Hegseth compared AI vendors to Boeing, which does not dictate how its aircraft are used, according to CBS News.

Citing NPR, Business Times said a supply-chain risk label would require defense contractors to certify Claude is absent from their systems. It also reported that Claude is the only AI model running inside classified Pentagon networks. The Wall Street Journal has reported Claude was used in the operation to capture former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, ABC noted; NPR said that happened via Palantir and that Anthropic had not specifically authorized it. An Anthropic spokesperson told ABC it could not comment on specific operations. The Pentagon source told ABC that xAI's Grok is on board for classified use, while the other companies in the program are close.

## Thursday, February 26

Amodei published a statement refusing the demand. He said Anthropic was the first frontier AI company to put models on the government's classified networks and had given up several hundred million dollars in revenue by cutting off firms linked to the Chinese Communist Party. He wrote that the company has never objected to particular military operations.

The 2 exceptions were mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. On weapons, he argued that "today, frontier AI systems are simply not reliable enough to power fully autonomous weapons," and said the Department had not accepted Anthropic's offer of joint R&D on reliability. He said the Department will only contract with companies accepting "any lawful use," and called the supply-chain and Defense Production Act threats "inherently contradictory". His conclusion: "we cannot in good conscience accede to their request". He offered a smooth transition if the Department chose to offboard Anthropic.

Al Jazeera reported that Anthropic's Thursday statement said new contract language "framed as compromise was paired with legalese that would allow those safeguards to be disregarded at will". TechCrunch, citing the BBC, reported that Sam Altman sent OpenAI staff a memo on Thursday saying he shared the same "red lines".

## Friday, February 27

Trump posted on Truth Social that every federal agency should stop using Anthropic's technology immediately, giving the Pentagon and other agencies already relying on it six months to wind down. He added that "we don't want it, and will not do business with them again". Al Jazeera said the post came just over an hour before the Pentagon's deadline, and that Trump warned of "the Full Power of the Presidency to make them comply, with major civil and criminal consequences to follow" if Anthropic did not help with the phase-out.

Hegseth then posted on X that he had told the Department of War to label Anthropic a supply-chain risk: "Effective immediately, no contractor, supplier, or partner that does business with the United States military may conduct any commercial activity with Anthropic". He called Anthropic's stance "fundamentally incompatible with American principles". The deadline passed with no agreement.

Anthropic said it was "deeply saddened" and would fight any designation in court: "We believe this designation would both be legally unsound and set a dangerous precedent for any American company that negotiates with the government". It also said it had "not yet received direct communication from the Department of War or the White House on the status of our negotiations".

Sen. Mark Warner, vice chair of the Senate intelligence committee, said the directive raises concerns about "whether national security decisions are being driven by careful analysis or political considerations". Altman told CNBC, "For all the differences I have with Anthropic, I mostly trust them as a company, and I think they really do care about safety". Ilya Sutskever wrote on X: "It's extremely good that Anthropic has not backed down, and it's significant that OpenAI has taken a similar stance". Elon Musk wrote that Anthropic "hates Western civilization". TechCrunch reported that an open letter backing Anthropic's position drew signatures that week from over 60 OpenAI staff and 300 at Google.

Within hours of the order, OpenAI announced its own Pentagon deal, which Altman said kept prohibitions on domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons. TechCrunch, citing The New York Times, reported that OpenAI and the government began meeting on Wednesday.

## Also this week

**Reported on February 23:** Anthropic said DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax generated over 16 million exchanges with Claude through about 24,000 fraudulent accounts to distill its capabilities. It counted over 150,000 exchanges from DeepSeek, over 3.4 million from Moonshot and over 13 million from MiniMax. When Anthropic released a new model mid-campaign, MiniMax shifted nearly half its traffic to it within 24 hours. One proxy network ran more than 20,000 fraudulent accounts at once, Anthropic said. The company argued the attacks strengthen the case for chip export controls. TechCrunch noted the Trump administration had last month allowed exports of chips like the H200 to China, and quoted Dmitri Alperovitch: "Now we know this for a fact". TechCrunch said it had reached out to the 3 labs for comment.

**Reported on February 28:** TechCrunch reported that Altman had announced late Friday an agreement letting the Department of Defense use OpenAI models on its classified network. Altman wrote: "Two of our most important safety principles are prohibitions on domestic mass surveillance and human responsibility for the use of force, including for autonomous weapon systems". He said OpenAI will build technical safeguards and deploy engineers with the Pentagon, and asked the department to offer the same terms to all AI companies. Fortune's Sharon Goldman reported Altman told staff that "if the model refuses to do a task, then the government would not force OpenAI to make it do that task".
