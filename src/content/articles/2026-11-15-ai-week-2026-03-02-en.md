*Written on November 15, 2026, covering news from Mar 2–8, 2026.*

OpenAI ran through nearly every major story of the week: 2 model releases, a loan of up to $40 billion sought by SoftBank largely to fund its OpenAI stake, and the resignation of its hardware lead over the Pentagon agreement. Amazon added €18 billion to its Spanish data center plans, and Apple opened pre-orders for the M5 MacBook Air.

## Monday, March 2

Amazon announced it will spend an additional €18 billion, or $21.05 billion, in Spain to expand data center capacity and its server supply chain. That comes on top of a €15.7 billion investment detailed in late 2024. A second report said the plan was unveiled at MWC in Barcelona and puts Amazon's total commitment in the Aragon region at €33.7bn.

The build-out centers on Aragón: Amazon plans data centers in Teruel alongside new facilities in Huesca and Zaragoza, which will give AWS servers in all 3 of the region's provinces once finished. That report added that AWS will be the first major tech company to build data centres in Teruel. Part of the new capacity will be dedicated to AI infrastructure.

Amazon will also build 3 supply chain sites for AWS: a server manufacturing plant, a logistics hub described as a manufacturing fulfillment warehouse, and a facility for producing and repairing AI servers.

On jobs, Amazon estimates about 29,900 direct, indirect and induced full-time equivalent positions, with 6,700 of them across its data center operations and the suppliers building the sites. The same report cited company estimates of a €31.7bn contribution to Spain's GDP by 2035, €18.5bn of it and 13,400 equivalent jobs in Aragon, plus around 1,800 direct jobs in Aragon from the new money. AWS also said its Aragon data centres have run on 100% renewable energy since 2022, and it has committed €30m to community programmes in the region through 2035 and invested €17.2 million in local water stewardship initiatives.

"We're supporting Europe's digital capabilities and expanding cloud and AI infrastructure that businesses and organizations are already using to grow and innovate," said David Zapolsky, Amazon's chief global affairs and legal officer. SiliconANGLE noted it is Amazon's second multibillion-dollar international investment since December, after plans to invest more than $35 billion in India through 2030.

## Tuesday, March 3

OpenAI updated its most popular ChatGPT model with GPT-5.3 Instant, available to all ChatGPT users. The company said the changes target tone, relevance and conversational flow, problems that don't show up in benchmarks. The model cuts back on refusals, on defensive or moralizing preambles and on dramatic interjections.

On accuracy, OpenAI says hallucinations on high-stakes queries in medicine, law and finance fell by 26.8% with web search and by 19.7% without it. In user-flagged conversations involving factual errors, hallucinations dropped 22.5% with web use and 9.6% without. WinBuzzer pointed out that OpenAI published only relative improvements, not absolute error-rate baselines.

Developers can call the model through the API as gpt-5.3-chat-latest, and paid subscribers keep access to GPT-5.2 Instant until June 3, 2026. OpenAI said its safety architecture is unchanged and only refusal calibration was adjusted. It also acknowledged that Japanese and Korean responses can still sound stilted, with tone improvements English-first for now. Updates to Thinking and Pro were promised for later.

## Wednesday, March 4

Apple, which announced the MacBook Air with M5 on March 3, opened pre-orders on March 4 in 33 countries and regions including the U.S., with deliveries and in-store availability starting March 11. Macworld later confirmed the laptop went on sale on March 11.

Both sizes now ship with 512GB as base storage, twice what the last generation offered, and for the first time buyers can option them to 4TB. The M5 chip pairs a 10-core CPU with an up-to-10-core GPU carrying a Neural Accelerator in each core, which Apple says delivers up to 4x faster AI performance than the M4 MacBook Air. Unified memory bandwidth rises to 153GB/s, a 28 percent gain over M4, and Apple's N1 chip adds Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6. Apple quotes up to 18 hours of battery life.

Launch pricing was $1,099 for the 13-inch ($999 for education) and $1,299 for the 15-inch ($1,199 for education). Macworld later reported that Apple raised prices by $200 in June 2026, to $1,299 and $1,499 respectively. In its review, Macworld found the fanless design limits sustained performance, with demanding games slowing after around 10 minutes.

## Thursday, March 5

OpenAI introduced GPT-5.4, pitched as its most capable and efficient frontier model. The standard model serves the API and developer tools, GPT-5.4 Thinking appears in ChatGPT, and GPT-5.4 Pro targets users who want maximum performance on complex work. The API version supports up to 1 million tokens of context.

Cyber Security News described it as OpenAI's first general-purpose model with native computer use, letting agents operate software via screenshots, mouse commands and keyboard input. It reported a 75.0% success rate on OSWorld-Verified, above a human benchmark of 72.4% and GPT-5.2's 47.3%, plus 83.0% on GDPval versus 70.9% for GPT-5.2. OpenAI says that versus GPT-5.2, the chance of a false individual claim drops by 33% and the chance of an error somewhere in a full response drops by 18%.

A new Tool Search feature lets the model fetch tool definitions only when needed rather than loading every one into the prompt. In ChatGPT, GPT-5.4 Thinking can show an upfront reasoning plan that users can redirect mid-response. OpenAI also said deception is less likely in GPT-5.4 Thinking, "suggesting that the model lacks the ability to hide its reasoning and that CoT monitoring remains an effective safety tool".

Mercor CEO Brendan Foody said the model "excels at creating long-horizon deliverables such as slide decks, financial models, and legal analysis". GPT-5.4 Thinking is available to ChatGPT Plus, Team and Pro subscribers and will replace GPT-5.2 Thinking over 3 months.

## Friday, March 6

Bloomberg News reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that SoftBank is seeking a loan of up to $40 billion, mainly to fund its investment in OpenAI. The bridge loan would run roughly 12 months, with 4 lenders including JPMorgan underwriting it; talks are ongoing and terms could change. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report.

THE DECODER noted that if completed, it would be the largest pure dollar borrowing in SoftBank's history. SoftBank held about 11% of OpenAI at the end of last year, and CEO Masayoshi Son has made an "all in" bet on the company. Last month, OpenAI said it would raise $110 billion at a possible $840 billion valuation, with SoftBank and Nvidia putting in $30 billion apiece and Amazon $50 billion. THE DECODER, citing the Financial Times, added that OpenAI's partners have accumulated around $96 billion in combined debt.

## Saturday, March 7

Caitlin Kalinowski resigned from leading OpenAI's hardware team, citing the company's agreement with the Department of Defense. "AI has an important role in national security," she wrote, adding that "surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got". In a follow-up post on X she called it "a governance concern first and foremost". She said the decision was "about principle, not people" and expressed "deep respect" for CEO Sam Altman.

An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed her departure, and the company said its red lines are "no domestic surveillance and no autonomous weapons". TechCrunch reported the Pentagon deal came after the Pentagon's talks with Anthropic fell apart and the Pentagon designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk, which Anthropic said it will fight in court. ChatGPT uninstalls surged 295%, and as of Saturday afternoon Claude and ChatGPT were the No. 1 and No. 2 free apps on the U.S. App Store. Kalinowski joined OpenAI in November 2024 after leading AR glasses work at Meta.
