A federal judge sided with Anthropic against the Trump administration. OpenAI pulled the plug on its Sora app. A new pro-AI political group said it plans to spend more than $100 million in the 2026 midterms.

## Monday, March 23

Apple said its 37th Worldwide Developers Conference will open on Monday, June 8 and close on Friday, June 12. As in 2025, it will be mostly online, open to every developer and free of charge. The keynote opens day one, June 8, at 10:00 Pacific Time, where Apple will show new versions of iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, tvOS and visionOS, all numbered 27.

Apple said this year's event will focus on "AI advancements" alongside platform updates and new developer tools. TechCrunch noted that AI went largely unmentioned at last year's conference, which centred on the "Liquid Glass" design, and that Apple signed a deal with Google earlier this year to use Gemini for AI features on its platform. 9to5Mac expects Apple to refine Liquid Glass, ship Siri and Apple Intelligence features powered by Gemini models, and work on overall stability.

There is also an in-person day on June 8 at Apple Park in Cupertino for select developers and students, chosen by random lottery. Entries close on Monday, March 30, with the cutoff set at 11:59 p.m. Pacific Time, and Apple says it will tell those selected on April 2. The keynote and sessions will stream on the Apple Developer app, Apple's website and YouTube, and in China on the Apple Developer Bilibili channel.

Susan Prescott, Apple's vice president of Worldwide Developer Relations, said: "We can’t wait to see many of you online and in person for what is sure to be one of our best WWDC events yet." Per 9to5Mac, first developer betas usually land after the day-one keynote, public betas follow in July, and the official release comes in September.

## OpenAI closes Sora

OpenAI said in a social media post on Tuesday that it was "saying goodbye to the Sora app" and that it would soon explain how users can keep what they made. "What you made with Sora mattered, and we know this news is disappointing," the company added.

OpenAI had launched Sora as a standalone app in September 2025. Advocacy groups, academics and experts raised alarms about nonconsensual images and realistic deepfakes spreading on it, and OpenAI restricted content featuring public figures only after pushback from family estates and an actors' union.

Disney had signed a three-year licensing pact with OpenAI just 3 months before the shutdown, opening more than 200 of its characters, including Marvel, Pixar and Star Wars ones, to Sora users. In a Tuesday statement, Disney said it respects "OpenAI’s decision to exit the video generation business and to shift its priorities elsewhere" and that it will keep engaging with AI platforms. OpenAI said ChatGPT's image generation is not affected.

## Thursday, March 26

Judge Rita Lin, a federal district judge in the Northern District of California, granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction in its suit against the Trump administration. The order bars the administration from implementing or enforcing President Donald Trump's directive banning federal agencies from using Claude, and it hampers the Pentagon's effort to brand Anthropic a threat to national security.

Lin did not mince words. "Punishing Anthropic for bringing public scrutiny to the government's contracting position is classic illegal First Amendment retaliation," she wrote. She added: "Nothing in the governing statute supports the Orwellian notion that an American company may be branded a potential adversary and saboteur of the U.S. for expressing disagreement with the government." At a 90-minute hearing two days earlier, on Tuesday, she had questioned why the government punished the company.

The background: Anthropic signed a $200 million Pentagon contract in July, but talks stalled in September over deploying Claude on the GenAI.mil platform. The Defense Department wanted access for all lawful purposes, while Anthropic wanted assurances against fully autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth declared Anthropic a supply chain risk in an X post in late February, and shortly before that Trump told agencies on Truth Social to "immediately cease" using its technology, with a six-month phase-out for agencies like the DOD. CNBC reports Anthropic is the first American company publicly given that label, which requires contractors including Amazon, Microsoft and Palantir to certify they do not use Claude in military work.

The limits matter. Lin wrote that her order is delayed for a week and neither forces the Pentagon to use Anthropic's products nor stops it moving to other AI providers. A separate, narrower case over a different statute is pending at the federal appeals court in Washington, D.C.. A final verdict could be months away.

Anthropic said it was "grateful to the court for moving swiftly, and pleased they agree Anthropic is likely to succeed on the merits." Briefs supporting Anthropic came from Catholic theologians, retired U.S. military leaders, rank-and-file tech staff, trade groups and Microsoft. The Pentagon did not immediately respond to Military.com's request for comment.

## Saturday, March 28

Bluesky launched Attie, a standalone app in which users describe the feed they want in plain language and AI builds it, WinBuzzer reports. WinBuzzer dates the unveiling at the Atmosphere conference by Jay Graber and CTO Paul Frazee to March 29. Attie runs on Anthropic's Claude and the AT Protocol.

Graber, formerly CEO and now chief innovation officer, built it with a new Exploration team, while True Ventures partner Toni Schneider serves as interim CEO. "We think AI should serve people, not platforms," Graber said in her announcement. Attie is in closed beta for conference attendees, with a waiting list at attie.ai. Feeds stay in Attie for now, with plans to extend them to Bluesky and other AT Protocol apps later.

Bluesky says a funding round that wrapped up last year brought in an extra $100 million, which Schneider said gives it more than 3 years of runway; he also said no crypto integration is planned. Bluesky has 43.4 million users, though WinBuzzer reports daily mobile users fell to about 3.6 million. Whether Attie will charge a fee has not been decided.

## Also this week

"President Trump has made it clear, America will win the AI race against China, period," Budowich told Fox News Digital. Fox, citing Axios, reports the group built a scorecard ranking lawmakers on alignment with Trump's AI agenda. The field is crowded: Leading the Future has reported raising $50 million, and Meta backs a super PAC expected to spend roughly $65 million, mostly on state races.
